# MS-4004.X — Customer Service Hands-On Lab
### Acme Bottling · Microsoft 365 Copilot

**Session:** 4 of 7 · Customer Service (ACs)
**Audience:** Account coordinators and customer service team members who already use
Copilot and want to move faster on threads, handoffs, and customer replies.
**Format:** ~60 minutes of hands-on (up to ~75 if your group goes deep), inside a
~90-minute session.
**Level:** Intermediate. We skip the basics and get straight into real Acme Bottling work.

You'll take a long, tangled customer email thread and a shipment export, get to the point
fast, protect a clean handoff before PTO, and draft a customer-ready reply — and leave
with reusable prompts.

> **How to use this lab:** Just read and follow along. Nothing to install, no repo to
> download, no code to save. Open the files linked below and go.

---

## What you'll be able to do

- Summarize a long customer thread into issue, status, owner, and next action.
- Cross-check a customer's story against the shipment data to build an exception list.
- Draft a clean PTO handoff so nothing drops when you're out.
- Write a customer-ready response that's accurate and on-tone.
- Build reusable prompts for summaries, exception lists, handoffs, and replies.

## Learning arc

This lab moves in one direction: **find the truth, then act on it.**

|  | **Exercise 1 — Get to the point fast** | **Exercise 2 — Protect the handoff and reply** |
|---|---|---|
| **You're doing** | Cutting a tangled thread down to facts | Handing off cleanly and answering the customer |
| **Copilot's job** | Summarize, cross-check, prioritize | Draft to a structure, in your tone |
| **Your job** | Decide what's confirmed vs. claimed | Decide what's safe to promise |
| **Stakes** | Internal — only you see it | External — a colleague and the customer see it |
| **You leave with** | The real issue and a priority order | Two artifacts + a 4-prompt library |

Every task runs the same beat: **prompt → read it critically → keep what's reusable.**
Every exercise ends the same way: **Source / Assumptions / Risk / Human check.**

## The one rule to protect

**Copilot assists. A human validates.** At the end of every exercise you'll name the
source Copilot used, the assumptions it made, the risk, and what you must check before
anything goes to a customer. A summary is a starting point, not a confirmed fact.

# Exercise 1 — Get to the point fast (about 30 min)

**Scenario:** You're covering the Blue Ridge Springs account. There's a long thread about
a late bottle shipment (ORD-5006) and a nervous customer. Cut through it.

### Task 1 — Set your service voice and clean your context (8 min)

1. First, tune Copilot for this work. In **Copilot Chat** settings, add a **custom
   instruction**:

   ```
   When I work on customer accounts: be accurate before you're reassuring. Separate what
   the customer claimed from what our data confirms. Never invent a date, a cause, or a
   commitment. Flag anything I still need to verify before I reply.
   ```

2. In OneDrive, create a folder named **`Acme - Blue Ridge Springs`**.
3. Save the customer thread and the shipment export into it.
4. Work from that folder / a fresh chat so Copilot references only Blue Ridge material.

> **Why:** One folder, one clean chat per customer keeps summaries accurate. The custom
> instruction is what stops Copilot from smoothing over a fact you haven't confirmed.

### Task 2 — Summarize the thread (10 min)

1. Open **Copilot Chat**, attach `AcmeBottling_Customer_Thread.md`, and paste:

   ```
   Summarize this customer email thread in a tight brief: the core issue, current status,
   who owns the next step, the customer's deadline, the business impact they stated, and
   the single most important next action. Keep it under 150 words. Do not invent details
   that aren't in the thread.
   ```

2. Then separate claim from confirmed fact:

   ```
   Now split that summary into two lists: (a) facts stated in the thread by Acme, and
   (b) claims made by the customer that I have not verified against our systems. Do not
   merge them.
   ```

3. You now have the thread boiled down — and you know which half still needs checking.

### Task 3 — Cross-check against the shipment data (6 min)

1. Open `AcmeBottling_Orders_Shipments.xlsx` and find ORD-5006.
2. In Copilot Chat, paste:

   ```
   Based on the thread, order ORD-5006 for Blue Ridge Springs is on hold due to a credit
   hold on Acme's side, not a production issue, and the customer needs bottles by the 25th
   or they lose a production day (about 12,000 units). List the open exceptions and what
   must happen, in priority order, to resolve this before the deadline.
   ```

3. Confirm the priority list matches what the spreadsheet actually shows for that order.

### Validation checkpoint (6 min)

- **Source:** Did Copilot use only the thread and the order data you provided?
- **Assumptions:** Did it infer any cause or date that isn't stated?
- **Risk:** What breaks if you act on the wrong root cause (production vs. credit hold)?
- **Human check:** Which fact will you personally confirm before replying to the customer?

> **Exercise 1 output:** a 150-word thread brief, a claimed-vs-confirmed split, and a
> priority-ordered exception list.

# Exercise 2 — Protect the handoff and reply (about 30 min)

**Scenario:** You're heading out on PTO tomorrow, and this can't drop. Build a clean
handoff and a customer-ready reply.

### Task 1 — Build a PTO handoff brief in Word (9 min)

1. Open a new **Word** doc in the Blue Ridge folder.
2. Open **Copilot in Word** and paste:

   ```
   Create a PTO handoff brief for a colleague covering the Blue Ridge Springs account
   while I'm out. Include: the open issue (ORD-5006 on credit hold), the customer's
   deadline (the 25th) and stated impact, who owns the next step, what's already been
   done, and exactly what my colleague needs to watch for and do. Under 200 words, plain
   English. End with an "If X happens, do Y" line for the deadline slipping.
   ```

3. Review. Confirm the "If X happens, do Y" contingency is there.

### Task 2 — Draft a customer-ready reply in Outlook (8 min)

1. Open **Outlook**, start a reply, open **Copilot**.
2. Paste:

   ```
   Draft a reply to Sarah Kettner at Blue Ridge Springs about order ORD-5006. Be honest
   and reassuring: acknowledge the impact to their fall run, explain we're clearing an
   internal hold (keep it brief, don't over-share), and commit to confirming a firm ship
   date by end of day. Friendly, direct, under 130 words. Leave a placeholder for the
   confirmed ship date.
   ```

3. Read for tone. Drop in the real date once confirmed.

### Task 3 — Check what you promised (6 min)

1. Paste your draft reply back into the clean chat:

   ```
   Review this draft reply. List every commitment it makes to the customer — dates,
   quantities, causes, next steps. For each, say whether the thread or the order data
   actually supports it, or whether it's something I still have to confirm. Flag anything
   that over-promises.
   ```

2. Remove or soften anything you can't stand behind. **This is a first pass, not an
   approval** — a human still owns the send.

### Task 4 — Save your prompt library (3 min)

Create a Word doc titled **"Acme Customer Service — Prompt Library"** and paste in the
four prompts from the [Prompt Library](#prompt-library-your-leave-behind) section below.

This is your leave-behind. Swap in any customer and order next week.

### Validation checkpoint (4 min)

- **Source:** Did the reply and handoff use only confirmed facts from this account?
- **Assumptions:** Did Copilot soften or overstate anything about the cause or timeline?
- **Risk:** What's the cost of sending a firm date you haven't actually confirmed?
- **Human check:** Who reviews the customer reply before it goes out?

> **Exercise 2 output:** a PTO handoff brief with a contingency line, a customer reply
> with every commitment checked, and a 4-prompt library.

## Prompt Library (your leave-behind)

Save these four. Fill the `[placeholders]` each time.

**1. Thread summary**
```
Summarize this customer thread in a tight brief: core issue, current status, who owns the
next step, the customer's deadline, the stated business impact, and the single most
important next action. Under [WORD COUNT]. Do not invent details. Then split it into
facts we stated versus claims I have not verified. Thread: [PASTE]
```

**2. Exception & priority list**
```
Using [SOURCE FILE] and the facts below, list the open exceptions on [ORDER/ACCOUNT] and
what must happen, in priority order, to resolve them before [DEADLINE]. Facts: [PASTE]
```

**3. PTO handoff brief**
```
Create a PTO handoff brief for a colleague covering [ACCOUNT]. Include: the open issue,
the customer's deadline and stated impact, who owns the next step, what's already been
done, and exactly what to watch for. Under [WORD COUNT], plain English. End with an
"If X happens, do Y" line.
```

**4. Customer reply (with promise check)**
```
Draft a reply to [CONTACT] at [CUSTOMER] about [ORDER]. Honest and reassuring:
acknowledge the impact, explain the cause briefly without over-sharing, and commit to
[NEXT STEP]. Under [WORD COUNT]. Leave placeholders for anything unconfirmed.
Then list every commitment the draft makes and whether my data actually supports it.
```

## Facilitator notes

- **Data hygiene:** The clean-folder step (Ex.1 Task 1) is the teachable habit — one
  customer, one folder, one clean chat.
- **Service reality:** The teachable moment is Ex.2 Task 3. Copilot writes a *reassuring*
  reply by default, and reassuring drifts into promising. Having it audit its own
  commitments is what keeps an unconfirmed ship date from reaching a customer.
- **Capture agent ideas:** If someone says "I wish every incoming thread got auto-triaged
  and matched to its order," that's a real agent opportunity — capture it and route to
  **Chris Bartlow**. Keep this lab current-state Copilot.
- **Timing:** Fast groups can rerun the summary against a second account (Cascade Craft or
  Summit Sparkling) from the same orders export.
