# MS-4004.X — Customer Service Hands-On Lab
### Acme Bottling · Microsoft 365 Copilot

**Audience:** Account coordinators and customer service team members who already use Copilot and want to move faster on threads, handoffs, and customer replies.

**Format:** ~60 minutes of hands-on

**Level:** Intermediate. We skip the basics and get straight into real Acme Bottling work.

You'll take a long, tangled customer email thread and a shipment export, get to the point fast, protect a clean handoff before PTO, and draft a customer-ready reply — and leave with reusable prompts.

> **How to use this lab:** Just read and follow along. Nothing to install, no repo to download, no code to save. Open the files linked below and go.

---

## What you'll be able to do

- Summarize a long customer thread into issue, status, owner, and next action.
- Cross-check a customer's story against the shipment data to build an exception list.
- Draft a clean PTO handoff so nothing drops when you're out.
- Write a customer-ready response that's accurate and on-tone.
- Build reusable prompts for summaries, exception lists, handoffs, and replies.
- Keep a validation habit so no unconfirmed commitment reaches a customer.

## Learning arc

This lab moves in one direction: **find the truth, then act on it.**

|  | **Exercise 1 — Get to the point fast** | **Exercise 2 — Protect the handoff and reply** |
|---|---|---|
| **You're doing** | Cutting a tangled thread down to facts | Handing off cleanly and answering the customer |
| **Copilot's job** | Summarize, cross-check, prioritize | Draft to a structure, in your tone |
| **Your job** | Decide what's confirmed vs. claimed | Decide what's safe to promise |
| **Stakes** | Internal — only you see it | External — a colleague and the customer see it |
| **You leave with** | The real issue and a priority order | Two artifacts + a 4-prompt library |

Every task runs the same beat: **prompt → read it critically → keep what's reusable.**
Every exercise ends the same way: **Source / Assumptions / Risk / Human check.**

## What this means before you reply to a customer

A summary is a starting point, not a confirmed fact — and a commitment you send is one you
own. Keep what the customer claimed separate from what Acme's data confirms, and verify
every date and quantity before it leaves your outbox. Every exercise ends with source,
assumptions, risk, and the human check.

# Exercise 1 — Get to the point fast

**Objective:** Reduce a long, tangled thread to a short brief, separate what the customer
claimed from what Acme's data confirms, and produce a priority-ordered exception list.

**Scenario:** You're covering the Blue Ridge Springs account. There's a long thread about
a late bottle shipment (ORD-5006) and a nervous customer. Cut through it.

### Task 1 — Set your service voice and clean your context

First you'll tell Copilot how to handle customer facts, then scope it to this account.

1. Open **Copilot Chat** and go to **Settings** (gear icon or your profile picture, top
   right) → **Personalization**.
2. Add this **custom instruction** and save:

   ```
   When I work on customer accounts: be accurate before you're reassuring. Separate what
   the customer claimed from what our data confirms. Never invent a date, a cause, or a
   commitment. Flag anything I still need to verify before I reply.
   ```

   *No Personalization option in your tenant? Paste this block at the top of your first
   prompt in each exercise instead.*
3. In Copilot Chat, start a **New chat** (the pencil or **New chat** icon at the top of
   the pane) so nothing from other accounts bleeds in.

> **Why:** One folder, one clean chat per customer keeps summaries accurate. The custom
> instruction is what stops Copilot from smoothing over a fact you haven't confirmed.

### Task 2 — Summarize the thread

Summarizing is the easy half. The real exercise is step 3, where you make Copilot split its
own summary in two — because a good summary reads as uniformly true, and half of this one
isn't verified yet.

1. In your clean **Copilot Chat**, attach `AcmeBottling_Customer_Thread.md` using the
   paperclip icon next to the prompt box.
2. Paste this prompt and press Enter:

   ```
   Summarize this customer email thread in a tight brief: the core issue, current status,
   who owns the next step, the customer's deadline, the business impact they stated, and
   the single most important next action. Keep it under 150 words. Do not invent details
   that aren't in the thread.
   ```

3. **Stay in the same chat** and separate claim from confirmed fact:

   ```
   Now split that summary into two lists: (a) facts stated in the thread by Acme, and
   (b) claims made by the customer that I have not verified against our systems. Do not
   merge them.
   ```

4. You now have the thread boiled down — and you know which half still needs checking.

### Task 3 — Cross-check against the shipment data

Now you take the unverified half and put it against Acme's record. Note what the prompt
does: it states the facts *you* confirmed in the sheet, then asks for priority — it doesn't
ask Copilot to work out the root cause on its own.

1. Open `AcmeBottling_Orders_Shipments.xlsx` in **Excel** — press **Start** and type
   *Excel*, or use the app launcher (the grid icon, top-left) in Microsoft 365 on the web —
   and find **ORD-5006**.
2. **Go back to the same chat** and paste:

   ```
   Based on the thread, order ORD-5006 for Blue Ridge Springs is on hold due to a credit
   hold on Acme's side, not a production issue, and the customer needs bottles by the 25th
   or they lose a production day (about 12,000 units). List the open exceptions and what
   must happen, in priority order, to resolve this before the deadline.
   ```

3. Confirm the priority list matches what the spreadsheet actually shows for that order.

### Validation checkpoint

- **Source:** Did Copilot use only the thread and the order data you provided?
- **Assumptions:** Did it infer any cause or date that isn't stated?
- **Risk:** What breaks if you act on the wrong root cause (production vs. credit hold)?
- **Human check:** Which fact will you personally confirm before replying to the customer?

> **Exercise 1 output:** a 150-word thread brief, a claimed-vs-confirmed split, and a
> priority-ordered exception list.

# Exercise 2 — Protect the handoff and reply

**Objective:** Produce a handoff a colleague could act on cold, and a customer reply where
every commitment has been checked against your data.

**Scenario:** You're heading out on PTO tomorrow, and this can't drop. Build a clean
handoff and a customer-ready reply.

### Task 1 — Build a PTO handoff brief in Word

A handoff is written for someone who wasn't in the thread. That's why the prompt ends with
an "If X happens, do Y" line — a colleague covering cold can follow a status update, but
only a contingency tells them what to do when the date slips at 4pm on a Friday.

1. Open **Word** — press **Start** and type *Word*, or use the app launcher (the grid icon,
   top-left) in Microsoft 365 on the web. Choose **Blank document**, then **Create a copy →
   Create a copy online** → your **`Acme - Blue Ridge Springs`** folder → name it
   **`Blue Ridge PTO Handoff`**.
2. On the **Home** tab, click the **Copilot** button (lower right corner or right of the
   ribbon), then paste:

   ```
   Create a PTO handoff brief for a colleague covering the Blue Ridge Springs account
   while I'm out. Include: the open issue (ORD-5006 on credit hold), the customer's
   deadline (the 25th) and stated impact, who owns the next step, what's already been
   done, and exactly what my colleague needs to watch for and do. Under 200 words, plain
   English. End with an "If X happens, do Y" line for the deadline slipping.
   ```

3. **Where the answer lands depends on your version of Word.** Some builds draft straight
   into the document — if so, review it and click **Keep it** (or **Regenerate** /
   **Discard**). If the answer stays in the Copilot pane instead, click **Insert** under the
   response, or **Copy** and paste it into the page. Either way, nothing is final until you
   accept it.
4. Review. Confirm the "If X happens, do Y" contingency is there, then **save**.

### Task 2 — Draft a customer-ready reply in Outlook

This is the first thing a customer sees. Read the prompt closely: it asks for a *placeholder*
where the ship date goes. Copilot will happily write a firm date if you let it — and it has
no way of knowing whether that date is true.

1. Open **Outlook** — press **Start** and type *Outlook*, or use the app launcher (the grid
   icon, top-left) in Microsoft 365 on the web — and start a reply to the customer thread.
2. Click the **Copilot** button on the message toolbar and choose **Draft with Copilot**.
3. Paste:

   ```
   Draft a reply to Sarah Kettner at Blue Ridge Springs about order ORD-5006. Be honest
   and reassuring: acknowledge the impact to their fall run, explain we're clearing an
   internal hold (keep it brief, don't over-share), and commit to confirming a firm ship
   date by end of day. Friendly, direct, under 130 words. Leave a placeholder for the
   confirmed ship date.
   ```

4. Read for tone. Drop in the real date once confirmed.

### Task 3 — Check what you promised

The most valuable prompt in this lab. Copilot writes a *reassuring* reply by default, and
reassuring drifts into promising without anyone deciding it should. Here you make it audit
its own draft, commitment by commitment.

1. Copy your draft reply.
2. Go back to your clean **Copilot Chat**, paste the draft into the prompt box, and add:

   ```
   Review this draft reply. List every commitment it makes to the customer — dates,
   quantities, causes, next steps. For each, say whether the thread or the order data
   actually supports it, or whether it's something I still have to confirm. Flag anything
   that over-promises.
   ```

3. Remove or soften anything you can't stand behind. **This is a first pass, not an
   approval** — a human still owns the send.

### Task 4 — Save your prompt library

The reply is for Blue Ridge. The prompts are for every account you cover.

1. Create a new **Word** doc titled **"Acme Customer Service — Prompt Library"**.
2. Paste in the four prompts from the
   [Prompt Library](#prompt-library-your-leave-behind) section below.
3. Save it somewhere you'll actually find it again.

This is your leave-behind. Swap in any customer and order next week.

### Validation checkpoint

- **Source:** Did the reply and handoff use only confirmed facts from this account?
- **Assumptions:** Did Copilot soften or overstate anything about the cause or timeline?
- **Risk:** What's the cost of sending a firm date you haven't actually confirmed?
- **Human check:** Who reviews the customer reply before it goes out?

> **Exercise 2 output:** a PTO handoff brief with a contingency line, a customer reply
> with every commitment checked, and a 4-prompt library.

## Prompt Library (your leave-behind)

Save these four. Anything in `[BRACKETS]` is a blank you fill in — delete the brackets too.
If you're stuck on one, use what you did in this lab: `[CUSTOMER]` → *Blue Ridge Springs*,
`[ORDER]` → *ORD-5006*, `[DEADLINE]` → *the 25th*, `[WORD COUNT]` → *150 words*. Where it
says **PASTE**, paste the actual text in.

**1. Thread summary**
```
Summarize this customer thread in a tight brief: core issue, current status, who owns the
next step, the customer's deadline, the stated business impact, and the single most
important next action. Under [WORD COUNT]. Do not invent details. Then split it into
facts we stated versus claims I have not verified. Thread: [PASTE THREAD HERE]
```
*The split is the point, not the summary. Run it as one prompt and the two halves stay
separate; run it as two and Copilot will quietly re-merge them.*

**2. Exception & priority list**
```
Using [SOURCE FILE] and the facts below, list the open exceptions on [ORDER/ACCOUNT] and
what must happen, in priority order, to resolve them before [DEADLINE]. Say which ones you
can't rank because data is missing. Facts: [PASTE CONFIRMED FACTS HERE]
```
*Paste facts **you** confirmed, not the thread. If you paste the customer's version,
you've just asked Copilot to prioritize their story instead of your data.*

**3. PTO handoff brief**
```
Create a PTO handoff brief for a colleague covering [ACCOUNT]. Include: the open issue,
the customer's deadline and stated impact, who owns the next step, what's already been
done, and exactly what to watch for. Under [WORD COUNT], plain English. End with an
"If [WHAT MIGHT GO WRONG] happens, do [WHAT THEY SHOULD DO]" line.
```
*Write the contingency for the thing you're most afraid of. That's the one your colleague
won't know how to handle.*

**4. Customer reply (with promise check)**
```
Draft a reply to [CONTACT] at [CUSTOMER] about [ORDER]. Honest and reassuring:
acknowledge the impact, explain the cause briefly without over-sharing, and commit to
[NEXT STEP]. Under [WORD COUNT]. Leave placeholders for anything unconfirmed.
Then list every commitment the draft makes — dates, quantities, causes, next steps — and
whether my data actually supports each one. Flag anything that over-promises.
```
*Keep the second half attached to the first. Separating them is how an unconfirmed ship
date reaches a customer.*
