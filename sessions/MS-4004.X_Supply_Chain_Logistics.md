# MS-4004.X — Supply Chain / Logistics Hands-On Lab
### Acme Bottling · Microsoft 365 Copilot

**Audience:** Supply chain and logistics team members who already use Copilot and want to work faster on shipment status, exceptions, and escalations.

**Format:** ~60 minutes of hands-on

**Level:** Intermediate. We skip the basics and get straight into real Acme Bottling work.

You'll start from a spreadsheet exported from Acme's ERP — exactly like your real Monday morning — and leave with reusable prompts you can drop into your own reports.

> **How to use this lab:** Just read and follow along. Nothing to install, no repo to download, no code to save. Open the files linked below and go.

---

## What you'll be able to do

- Analyze an order/shipment export to find delays, exceptions, and missing information.
- Decide who owns the next step — Acme, a supplier, a customer, or the carrier.
- Turn a messy export into a clear status briefing for a manager.
- Build reusable prompts for report analysis, exception ID, status briefings, and
  escalations.
- Keep your Copilot work clean and trustworthy with a simple validation habit.

## Learning arc

This lab moves in one direction: **find the truth, then act on it.**

|  | **Exercise 1 — Find what's slipping** | **Exercise 2 — Turn findings into action** |
|---|---|---|
| **You're doing** | Reading a messy ERP export | Producing a briefing and an escalation |
| **Copilot's job** | Summarize, group, flag gaps | Draft in your voice, to a structure |
| **Your job** | Decide what's real and who owns it | Decide what's safe to send |
| **Stakes** | Internal — only you see it | External — your manager and a supplier see it |
| **You leave with** | A delay picture in business terms | Two artifacts + a 4-prompt library |

Every task runs the same beat: **prompt → read it critically → keep what's reusable.**
Every exercise ends the same way: **Source / Assumptions / Risk / Human check.**

## What this means when you commit to a date

Copilot can rank delays and name a likely owner, but "delay owner" and root cause are
inferences drawn from an export — not confirmed facts. Check with the carrier, plant, or
supplier before you promise a customer a new date. Every exercise ends with source,
assumptions, risk, and the human check.

# Exercise 1 — Find what's slipping

**Objective:** Turn a raw ERP export into a ranked list of delayed orders, in business
terms, with every data gap flagged instead of guessed.

**Scenario:** It's Monday. You've exported open and recent orders from Acme's ERP. A few
customers are asking where their bottles are. Let's find the problems fast.

### Task 1 — Set your operations voice and get a clean read

First you'll tell Copilot how to behave for this kind of work, then get an overview of the
export.

1. Open **Copilot Chat** and go to **Settings** (gear icon or your profile picture, top
   right) → **Personalization**.
2. Add this **custom instruction** and save:

   ```
   When I work with operational data: be precise and plain-spoken. Never fill in a
   missing value — flag it instead. Separate what the data says from what you inferred.
   End with the assumptions you made and what I should verify before acting.
   ```

   *No Personalization option in your tenant? Paste this block at the top of your first
   prompt in each exercise instead.*
3. Open `AcmeBottling_Orders_Shipments.xlsx` from your **`Acme Supply Chain Lab`** folder
   in **Excel** — press **Start** and type *Excel*, or use the app launcher (the grid icon,
   top-left) in Microsoft 365 on the web.
4. On the **Home** tab, click the **Copilot** button (lower right corner or right of the
   ribbon). A chat pane opens on the right.
5. Paste this prompt into the pane and press Enter:

   ```
   You are helping me review this order/shipment export. Give me a plain-English
   summary: how many orders total, how many are Delivered, In Transit, Delayed,
   Processing, or On Hold, and how many rows are missing a Status, Quantity, or
   PromisedDate. List the missing-data rows by OrderID. Do not guess missing values.
   ```

6. Read the summary. Notice Copilot flags the rows with blanks instead of filling them in.

> **Why this matters:** Real exports are messy. You want Copilot to surface gaps, not
> paper over them. The custom instruction makes that the default, not something you
> re-ask for every time.

### Task 2 — Find the delays and who owns them

The export already carries a `DelayOwner` field. The work here is making Copilot *group by
it* instead of narrating row after row — and then forcing it to commit to an order. Twelve
delays isn't a decision. Three names in priority order is.

1. **Stay in the same Excel Copilot pane** and paste:

   ```
   From this data, list every order where Status is Delayed or On Hold. For each, show
   OrderID, CustomerID, SKU, PromisedDate, DelayOwner, and ExceptionNote. Group them by
   DelayOwner (Acme, Supplier, Customer, Carrier). Which owner has the most delays?
   ```

2. You now have a delay picture sorted by who needs to act.
3. **Stay in the same Excel Copilot pane** and push once more — make Copilot commit to a
   priority:

   ```
   Of these, which three would you escalate first and why? Rank by customer impact and
   how overdue they are. Say which ones you can't rank because data is missing.
   ```

### Task 3 — Add customer and supplier context

Copilot only sees what you give it. The export carries IDs, not names — so until you hand
it the lookup, it can't tell you the delay you're chasing belongs to your biggest account
or sits behind a supplier who's already on the watch list.

1. Open `AcmeBottling_Master_Reference.xlsx` from your **`Acme Supply Chain Lab`** folder
   in **Excel** so you can see customer and supplier names on the **Customers** and
   **Suppliers** tabs.
2. **Go back to the same Excel Copilot pane** (or **Copilot Chat**) and paste:

   ```
   For the delayed and on-hold orders, help me translate IDs to names. Customer CUST-1001
   is Cascade Craft Beverages, CUST-1002 is Sunrise Juice Co., CUST-1004 is Blue Ridge
   Springs (Net 60), and CUST-1007 is Summit Sparkling. Supplier SUP-202 (PolyForm
   Resins) is flagged "at risk" and SUP-204 (GreenLeaf Packaging) is "delayed." Re-list my
   delays using names, and call out any delay tied to an at-risk or delayed supplier.
   ```

3. Now the story reads in business terms: which customers, which shaky suppliers.

### Validation checkpoint

Answer these four before moving on — this is the rule in action:

- **Source:** Which file(s) did Copilot use? (The ERP export; you supplied the name lookups.)
- **Assumptions:** Did it assume anything about blank rows or dates? Did it guess?
- **Risk:** What would go wrong if you sent this list as-is? (Missing statuses = blind spots.)
- **Human check:** Which 2–3 orders will you personally verify before escalating?

> **Exercise 1 output:** a ranked delay picture in business terms, with every data gap
> flagged rather than filled.

# Exercise 2 — Turn findings into action

**Objective:** Produce two things you could actually send — a manager status briefing and
a supplier escalation — and save the prompts that made them.

**Scenario:** Your manager wants a quick status read, and one delayed order needs to be
escalated to a supplier. Let's produce both — cleanly.

### Task 1 — Keep your context clean

Everything after this point is customer-specific and leaves your desk. Narrow the ground
Copilot is standing on before you generate a word of it.

1. Inside your **`Acme Supply Chain Lab`** folder in OneDrive, create a subfolder named
   **`Acme - Blue Ridge Springs`**.
2. Save a copy of the orders export and the reference workbook into it.
3. In **Copilot Chat**, start a **New chat** (the pencil or **New chat** icon at the top
   of the pane).
4. When you prompt about this customer, work from that subfolder and that fresh chat so
   Copilot references only Blue Ridge material — nothing bleeds in from other accounts.

> **Why:** One folder, one clean chat per customer keeps outputs accurate and easy to trust.

### Task 2 — Build a manager status briefing in Word

Your manager doesn't want the export, they want the read. Note that the prompt hands
Copilot the *structure* — four named sections — rather than asking it to decide what a
briefing looks like. That's the difference between a draft you tighten and a draft you
rewrite.

1. Open **Word** — press **Start** and type *Word*, or use the app launcher (the grid icon,
   top-left) in Microsoft 365 on the web. Choose **Blank document**, then **Create a copy →
   Create a copy online** → your **`Acme - Blue Ridge Springs`** subfolder → name it
   **`Blue Ridge Status Briefing`**.
2. On the **Home** tab, click the **Copilot** button (lower right corner or right of the
   ribbon), then paste:

   ```
   Draft a one-page operational status briefing for my manager about Blue Ridge Springs
   (CUST-1004). Use these facts: they have delayed and on-hold orders, one tied to a
   customer credit hold and one to a delayed packaging supplier (GreenLeaf). Structure it
   as: What's delayed, Who owns the next step, What needs escalation, What to monitor.
   Keep it under 250 words, plain English, no filler. End with an "Assumptions & to
   verify" line.
   ```

3. **Where the answer lands depends on your version of Word.** Some builds draft straight
   into the document — if so, review it and click **Keep it** (or **Regenerate** /
   **Discard**). If the answer stays in the Copilot pane instead, click **Insert** under the
   response, or **Copy** and paste it into the page. Either way, nothing is final until you
   accept it.
4. Review and tighten. Confirm the "Assumptions & to verify" line is there, then **save**.

### Task 3 — Draft an escalation email in Outlook

This one leaves the building. Watch what the prompt does *not* do: it never invents an
OrderID or a promised date. It asks for placeholders instead, because a confident wrong
number in a supplier escalation costs more than sending an hour later.

1. Open **Outlook** — press **Start** and type *Outlook*, or use the app launcher (the grid
   icon, top-left) in Microsoft 365 on the web — and start a new email.
2. Click the **Copilot** button on the message toolbar and choose **Draft with Copilot**.
3. Paste:

   ```
   Draft a short, professional escalation email to GreenLeaf Packaging (supplier SUP-204)
   about a delayed carton shipment affecting a Blue Ridge Springs order. Ask for a firm
   revised ship date and a reason for the delay. Friendly but direct, under 120 words.
   Leave a placeholder for the OrderID and promised date so I can fill them in.
   ```

4. Read it, drop in the real OrderID, and you've got an escalation ready to send after
   review.

### Task 4 — Save your prompt library

The briefing is for this week. The prompts are for every week after it.

1. Create a new **Word** doc titled **"Acme Supply Chain — Prompt Library"**.
2. Paste in the four prompts from the
   [Prompt Library](#prompt-library-your-leave-behind) section below.
3. Save it into your **`Acme Supply Chain Lab`** folder so you still have it after today.

This is your leave-behind. Adapt the names and SKUs to whatever you're working next week.

### Validation checkpoint

- **Source:** Did the briefing and email use only Blue Ridge facts from your clean folder?
- **Assumptions:** Anything Copilot inferred about dates, blame, or next steps?
- **Risk:** What's the cost of sending the escalation without checking the OrderID?
- **Human check:** Who signs off before this goes to the supplier?

> **Exercise 2 output:** a manager status briefing, a supplier escalation email, and a
> 4-prompt library you can reuse next Monday.

## Prompt Library (your leave-behind)

Save these four. Anything in `[BRACKETS]` is a blank you fill in — delete the brackets too.
If you're stuck on one, use what you did in this lab: `[SOURCE FILE]` →
*AcmeBottling_Orders_Shipments.xlsx*, `[STATUS FIELD]` → *Status*, `[OWNER FIELD]` →
*DelayOwner*, `[N]` → *three*. Where it says **PASTE**, paste the actual text in.

**1. Report analysis**
```
Review [SOURCE FILE] and give me a plain-English summary: row counts by [STATUS FIELD —
e.g. Status], and every row missing [REQUIRED FIELDS — e.g. Status, Quantity,
PromisedDate], listed by [ID FIELD — e.g. OrderID]. Do not guess missing values — flag
them.
```
*The last clause is the whole prompt. Drop "do not guess" and Copilot will quietly fill a
blank PromisedDate with something that looks entirely reasonable.*

**2. Delay & exception ID**
```
From [SOURCE FILE], list every record where [STATUS FIELD] is [EXCEPTION VALUES — e.g.
Delayed or On Hold]. Show [FIELDS TO DISPLAY], grouped by [OWNER FIELD — e.g. DelayOwner].
Tell me which owner has the most, and rank the top [N] to escalate by [WHAT YOU RANK ON —
e.g. customer impact and how overdue they are]. Say which ones you can't rank because data
is missing.
```
*Two dials worth turning: what counts as an exception, and what you rank on. Swap
"customer impact" for "revenue at risk" and the same prompt hands you a different top
three — which is the point.*

**3. Manager status briefing**
```
Draft a one-page operational status briefing for [AUDIENCE — e.g. my manager] about
[CUSTOMER/ACCOUNT]. Structure: What's delayed, Who owns the next step, What needs
escalation, What to monitor. Under [WORD COUNT], plain English, no filler. End with an
"Assumptions & to verify" line. Facts: [PASTE FACTS HERE]
```
*Naming the four sections yourself is what makes this reusable. If you let Copilot decide
the structure, you get a different briefing shape every Monday.*

**4. Escalation email**
```
Draft a short, professional escalation email to [SUPPLIER/PARTY] about [ISSUE] affecting
[CUSTOMER]. Ask for [SPECIFIC ASK — e.g. a firm revised ship date and a reason for the
delay]. Friendly but direct, under [WORD COUNT]. Leave placeholders for any ID or date I
need to confirm.
```
*"Leave placeholders" is deliberate, not lazy. A wrong OrderID in a supplier escalation is
a mistake with a paper trail.*
