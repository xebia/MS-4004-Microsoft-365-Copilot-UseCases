# MS-4004.X — Finance Hands-On Lab
### Acme Bottling · Microsoft 365 Copilot

**Audience:** Finance team members — analysts, accountants, FP&A, and finance managers — who already use Copilot and want to move faster from raw numbers to a reviewed story.

**Format:** ~70–85 minutes of hands-on, inside a ~90-minute session.

**Level:** Intermediate. We skip the basics and get straight into real Acme Bottling work.

Month-end just closed. You'll take an actuals export and find what moved and what looks wrong, scrub a messy AR aging sheet into a reporting-ready summary, and turn the result into a memo, a slide, and a regional email your CFO can act on.

> **How to use this lab:** Just read and follow along. Nothing to install, no repo to download, no code to save. Open the files linked below and go.

---

## What you'll be able to do

- Analyze budget vs actual to find variance drivers, anomalies, and missing values.
- Attribute those variances to vendor risk — cross supplier status and quality against the
  product lines they supply, and say plainly what the vendor data cannot tell you.
- Scrub a messy spreadsheet — bad dates, wrong buckets, negative or blank amounts — into a
  documented, corrected dataset instead of just a list of complaints.
- Turn the scrubbed data into a reporting summary: enriched with customer reference data,
  rolled up by region, and readable at a glance.
- Create finance content for three different audiences from one set of numbers — a CFO
  memo, a board slide, and a regional GM email — without changing the facts.
- Build reusable prompts for variance analysis, vendor risk, scrubbing, reporting, and
  content.
- Keep a validation habit so no unverified number reaches leadership as fact.

## Learning arc

This lab moves in one direction: **trust the numbers, then tell the story.**

|  | **Exercise 1 — From actuals to a reviewed story** | **Exercise 2 — Scrub the data, then report it** | **Exercise 3 — Turn the numbers into content** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **You're doing** | Finding drivers, anomalies, and the vendors behind them | Cleaning and summarizing AR data | Writing for three audiences |
| **Copilot's job** | Analyze, flag, attribute, question its own output | Propose fixes, build the rollup | Draft to a structure, label estimates |
| **Your job** | Decide which flags are real | Decide which fixes get applied | Decide which numbers are safe to state |
| **Stakes** | Internal — only you see it | Internal — but the report feeds everything after | External — the CFO and regional GMs see it |
| **You leave with** | A defensible variance list, plus vendor exposure with its limits stated | A cleaned sheet, a scrubbing rulebook, and an AR summary | A memo, a slide, an email, and an 8-prompt library |

Every task runs the same beat: **prompt → read it critically → keep what's reusable.**
Every exercise ends the same way: **Source / Assumptions / Risk / Human check.**

# Exercise 1 — From monthly actuals to a reviewed story

**Objective:** Produce a variance and anomaly list you could defend in a review — with the
arithmetic spot-checked, every unverifiable figure labeled, and each material variance
traced to the vendor behind it where the data supports it.

**Scenario:** Month-end close just finished. Your CFO, **Nora Whitfield**, wants the August
variance story before tomorrow's leadership sync — what moved, why, and what needs a
decision. She'll ask which of it is a vendor problem. You have the actuals export; you don't
yet trust every number in it.

### Task 1 — Set your finance voice and get oriented in Excel

First you'll tell Copilot how to behave with finance data, then get the shape of the file.

1. Open **Copilot Chat** and go to **Settings** (gear icon or your profile picture, top
   right) → **Personalization**.
2. Add this **custom instruction** and save:

   ```
   When I work with finance data: be precise and controls-first. Lead with the headline
   number and the biggest driver. Always separate confirmed facts from estimates, label
   anything unverified as "estimate pending review," and end with the assumptions you
   made and what a human should verify. Use plain English, no jargon.
   ```

   *No Personalization option in your tenant? Paste this block at the top of your first
   prompt in each exercise instead.* This one step changes the tone of everything below —
   you'll see Copilot flag its own assumptions without being asked each time.
3. Open `AcmeBottling_Finance_Monthly_Actuals.xlsx` in **Excel** — press **Start** and type
   *Excel*, or use the app launcher (the grid icon, top-left) in Microsoft 365 on the web.
   Note the two tabs: *Aug 2026 Actuals* and *YTD by Region*.
4. Click anywhere in the data and press **Ctrl+T** to format it as a table (accept the
   defaults). Copilot in Excel works much better on a real table.
5. On the **Home** tab, click the **Copilot** button (lower right corner or right of the
   ribbon), then paste:

   ```
   Give me a quick overview of this actuals table: columns, number of rows, total budget
   vs total actual revenue, and the overall variance %. Then list any rows where the
   Actual Revenue is blank or the Notes column is filled in.
   ```

6. Jot down what Copilot flags — you'll dig into those next.

> **Why this matters:** You're establishing the shape of the data before you ask it for
> conclusions. Skip this and you can't tell a real anomaly from a formatting artifact.

### Task 2 — Use Analyst to find variance drivers and anomalies

**Analyst** is a specialized agent that works through data step by step — writing and running
code against the file rather than pattern-matching an answer the way a normal chat prompt
does. Use it here because "largest unfavorable variance" is arithmetic, not prose, and
because you want the reasoning visible enough to spot-check in the next task.

Read the prompt before you paste it. It **defines what counts as an exception** — ±15%,
eight margin points — rather than leaving Copilot to decide what's notable. That's what makes
next month's run comparable to this one.

1. Open **Copilot Chat**, then open **Agents** in the left sidebar (or the agent picker
   above the prompt box) and select **Analyst**.
2. Attach `AcmeBottling_Finance_Monthly_Actuals.xlsx` using the paperclip icon.
3. Paste this analysis prompt — note it **defines what counts as an exception** so results
   are repeatable:

   ```
   Using ONLY the attached actuals file, analyze budget vs actual. Do three things:
   1) Identify the 3 largest unfavorable revenue variances and the 2 largest favorable
      ones, with region, product line, and variance %.
   2) Flag any anomalies: variances at or beyond +/-15%, gross-margin drops of 8 points
      or more, and any blank/missing actuals.
   3) For each flagged item, suggest one question I should ask before I trust it.
   End with the assumptions you made and which numbers you could not verify.
   ```

> **Model note:** if the reasoning looks shallow or the math seems off, pick a **more
> capable model** in the model picker near the prompt box and re-run. Finance analysis is a
> good case for the stronger reasoning model.

> **If Analyst isn't available in your tenant,** it's licensed separately from Copilot Chat
> in some setups. Run the same prompt in the **Excel Copilot pane** on the actuals workbook
> instead — you lose the step-by-step reasoning, but the thresholds and the
> "what-you-couldn't-verify" line still do their job, and Task 3 matters even more.

### Task 3 — Spot-check the flags before you trust them

This is the task that earns the whole exercise. Copilot states a variance percentage with
exactly the same confidence whether it computed it from two real figures or from one real
figure and one blank. Making it show the arithmetic is the only way to tell those apart.

1. Go back to the spreadsheet and verify **two** flagged rows by hand. Confirm the
   missing-actual row and the large Midwest miss both show up.
2. **Go back to the same Analyst chat** and make Copilot show its work:

   ```
   For the three unfavorable variances you flagged, show the exact budget figure, actual
   figure, and the arithmetic you used for each variance %. If any figure came from a
   blank or inferred value, say so explicitly.
   ```

3. If the arithmetic doesn't reconcile to the sheet, that's your finding — not Copilot's.

### Task 4 — Attribute the variances to vendor risk

A variance tells you *what* moved. Vendor operations asks *who outside the company moved
it.* The supplier file carries status and quality scores and nothing else — **no spend, no
payables, no purchase commitments, no contract terms** — so the last line of the prompt
matters as much as the first. Naming what the data can't answer is the finance-grade part.

1. **Stay in the same Analyst chat** and attach `AcmeBottling_Suppliers.csv` with the
   paperclip icon. The actuals file is already there.
2. Paste:

   ```
   Two attachments: our monthly actuals and our supplier list. Map each supplier to the
   product line it supplies, then cross that against the variance and margin findings you
   already produced. Return a table: product line, budget vs actual, gross-margin move, the
   supplier behind it, that supplier's status and quality score, and a Low/Med/High exposure
   rating with your reasoning in one line. List separately any product line with no supplier
   match and any supplier that maps to no product line — do not guess a mapping. Then state
   plainly what this data cannot tell me about vendor risk.
   ```

3. Check the mapping yourself against both files. **One product line has no supplier in the
   file at all** — confirm Copilot listed it as unmatched rather than quietly assigning it
   to the nearest plausible vendor.
4. Read the final paragraph closely. It should land somewhere near *no spend, no AP aging,
   no contract terms, no lead times.* If Copilot rated exposure without flagging those
   absences, it just took a risk position on data it doesn't have.
5. **Stay in the same chat** and turn it into something the CFO can act on:

   ```
   For the two suppliers not marked "On time," write one line each: what we would need to
   confirm before treating this as a financial exposure, and who owns that confirmation.
   Do not recommend a commercial action — procurement owns that call, not finance.
   ```

> **Why this matters:** "At risk" in a supplier file is a status someone typed, not a
> financial exposure. Turning one into the other needs spend and terms you don't have here.
> The skill being taught is saying so out loud instead of producing a confident rating
> anyway — and knowing where finance's read stops and procurement's decision starts.

### Validation checkpoint

In a scratch doc, answer for this analysis:

- **Source:** Which file/tab did each finding come from — actuals, supplier list, or both?
  Any row Copilot couldn't read?
- **Assumptions:** How did it treat the blank actual? Did it invent a supplier-to-product
  mapping, or list the unmatched line honestly?
- **Risk:** What's the cost if a flagged variance is wrong, a real one is missed, or a
  vendor is named as the cause without evidence?
- **Human check:** Who confirms these numbers before they reach the CFO — and who in
  procurement confirms the vendor read?

> **Exercise 1 output:** a variance and anomaly list with the arithmetic spot-checked and
> every unverifiable figure labeled, plus a vendor-exposure table that lists its unmatched
> rows and states the limits of the data behind it.

# Exercise 2 — Scrub the data, then report it

**Objective:** Catch the planted data-quality problems, decide and apply the fixes on your
terms, and turn the corrected sheet into an AR summary that's safe to report from.

**Scenario:** Nora won't look at an AR number until someone can say how it was cleaned. You
have a messy aging sheet and a customer reference list. You need a corrected dataset, a
written record of what you changed, and a regional rollup you can hand to anyone.

### Task 1 — Find the problems (propose, don't change)

Note the last line of the prompt: **do not change the data**. That's the control, not
politeness. Copilot proposes corrections; a named person decides which ones get applied and
signs their name to it.

1. Open `AcmeBottling_Finance_AR_Aging.xlsx` in **Excel** and go to the *AR Aging* tab.
2. Click anywhere in the data and press **Ctrl+T** to format it as a table.
3. On the **Home** tab, click the **Copilot** button (lower right corner or right of the
   ribbon), then paste:

   ```
   Review this AR aging sheet for data-quality problems ONLY within this file. Check for:
   inconsistent or invalid date formats, aging buckets that don't match Days Past Due,
   negative or zero amounts, blank Days Past Due, status typos, and possible duplicate
   invoices. Return a table: row, field, the issue, and the suggested fix. Do not change
   the data — just list the problems.
   ```

4. Confirm Copilot catches the big ones: the bad date format, the mis-bucketed 90+ invoice,
   the negative amount, the blank/negative days-past-due, and the status typo.

> **Why:** "Do not change the data" is the control. Copilot proposes; you decide what gets
> corrected and by whom.

### Task 2 — Scrub it: apply the fixes and write the rulebook

Now you turn that list into a *cleaned* dataset — and, more importantly, into rules you
can rerun next month.

1. Copy the *AR Aging* tab to a new tab named **AR Aging (Scrubbed)**. Never scrub in
   place; you want the original to reconcile back to.
2. Decide which of Copilot's proposed fixes you accept. Mark any you reject and why.
3. In the Copilot pane on the scrubbed tab, paste:

   ```
   Work only on this tab. For each fix I accepted below, give me the exact Excel formula
   or step to apply it — normalizing all Invoice Dates to a single format, recalculating
   the Aging Bucket from Days Past Due, and flagging (do not delete) negative amounts,
   blanks, and suspected duplicates in a new "QA Flag" column. Do not delete any row.
   Accepted fixes: [PASTE].
   ```

4. Apply the steps yourself and confirm the row count is unchanged and the total AR
   balance still ties to the original tab (any difference must be explainable).
5. **Stay in the same Excel Copilot pane** and ask Copilot to document what you just did:

   ```
   Write a short scrubbing rulebook for this sheet: each rule, the condition that triggers
   it, the correction applied, and whether it is automatic or needs human judgment. Add a
   final line stating which rules must never be automated.
   ```

   Keep that rulebook — it's the reusable asset, not the cleaned file.

> **Why this matters:** A clean number nobody can explain is worse than a dirty one.
> The rulebook is what makes next month's close faster and auditable.

### Task 3 — Build the AR reporting summary

A summary becomes a *report* the moment it declares what it left out. Two clauses do that
work here — "state exactly how much balance you excluded and why," and "list unmatched IDs
separately rather than guessing." Without them you get a total that looks complete and
isn't.

1. Open `AcmeBottling_Customers.csv` alongside the workbook. It carries region, segment,
   account manager, and payment terms for each `CustomerID`.
2. In the Copilot pane, on the scrubbed tab, paste:

   ```
   Using ONLY the scrubbed tab, build an AR reporting summary on a new tab: total
   outstanding and invoice count by aging bucket, and the top 5 customers by 90+ balance.
   Exclude any row where QA Flag is set, and state exactly how much balance you excluded
   and why. Show the formulas you used.
   ```

3. Enrich it. Paste in the relevant `AcmeBottling_Customers.csv` rows (or add them as a
   reference tab) and ask:

   ```
   Add region and account manager to the summary using the customer reference data I
   pasted. Roll up 90+ balance by region and by account manager. If a CustomerID has no
   match in the reference data, list it separately as unmatched — do not guess.
   ```

4. Ask for one chart: *"Add a simple column chart of 90+ balance by region, titled for a
   finance audience."*
5. Spot-check one region total by hand against the scrubbed rows. If it doesn't tie, that's
   your finding.

> **Why:** "Do not guess" plus a stated exclusion amount is what turns a summary into a
> report. Unmatched rows are a finding, not a rounding error.

### Validation checkpoint

- **Source:** Which tab did each number come from — original, scrubbed, or reference data?
- **Assumptions:** What did the scrub change, and what balance did the report exclude?
- **Risk:** What breaks downstream if a bucket was recalculated wrong?
- **Human check:** Who approves the scrubbing rulebook before it's reused?

> **Exercise 2 output:** a scrubbed AR tab, a scrubbing rulebook, and a regional AR summary
> with a chart and a stated exclusion.

# Exercise 3 — Turn the numbers into content

**Objective:** Create three pieces of finance content from one set of verified numbers —
a CFO memo, a board slide, and a regional GM email — with no unverified figure stated as
fact and no facts drifting between versions.

**Scenario:** The analysis is done and the AR data is clean. Nora needs a memo, the board
needs one slide, and the regional GMs need an email that lands differently without saying
anything different.

### Task 1 — Draft the finance memo in Word

Three references, one draft. The audience profile says who you're writing for, the format
doc says what shape it takes, and your findings file is the only place numbers may come
from. Separating those three is what stops the memo inventing a figure to fill a section.

1. Open `AcmeBottling_Leadership_Audience_Profile.docx` and
   `AcmeBottling_Executive_Memo_Format.docx` — read who you're writing for and the
   structure to match, then close them.

**Save your numbers so Word can read them.** Your figures are spread across two surfaces and
two exercises. Don't scroll back through them — make each surface hand you one block, and
copy twice.

2. **Go back to the same Analyst chat** from Exercise 1 and paste:

   ```
   Recap Exercise 1 as one block I can copy: the variance drivers and anomalies, the
   figures behind each with the tab they came from, the vendor-risk attribution, and
   anything you flagged as unverified. Keep every number and every caveat. Use headings
   and bullets. No preamble.
   ```

3. Copy that answer. Open **Word** — press **Start** and type *Word*, or use the app
   launcher (the grid icon, top-left) in Microsoft 365 on the web — choose **Blank
   document** and paste it in.
4. **Go back to the same Excel Copilot pane** from Exercise 2 and paste:

   ```
   Recap the AR work as one block I can copy: the scrubbing rulebook, the AR summary by
   aging bucket, the top 5 customers by 90+ balance, the regional and account-manager
   rollups, the exact balance excluded and why, and any unmatched CustomerIDs. Use
   headings and bullets. No preamble.
   ```

5. Copy that answer and paste it into the **same Word document**, below the variance
   findings. Then save: **Create a copy → Create a copy online** → your
   **`Acme - Finance Month-End`** folder → name it **`Month-End Findings`**.

   > **Why this one save matters:** Copilot's context stops at the app boundary — Word and
   > PowerPoint can't see your Analyst chat or the Excel pane, and those two can't see each
   > other. One file in your lab folder is the only thing all of them can read. From here on
   > you'll point Copilot at the findings instead of re-pasting them.

6. Create a new **Word** document, then **Create a copy → Create a copy online** → the same
   folder → name it **`Month-End CFO Memo`**. On the **Home** tab click the **Copilot**
   button (lower right corner or right of the ribbon).
7. In the prompt box type **`/`** and reference three files — **Month-End Findings**,
   **AcmeBottling_Leadership_Audience_Profile**, and **AcmeBottling_Executive_Memo_Format**
   — then add:

   ```
   Draft a one-page finance executive memo for the audience described in the referenced
   audience profile, following the referenced memo format exactly, using the referenced
   findings as the only source of numbers. Structure: 1) Bottom line (2–3 sentences),
   2) Variance highlights (table: area, budget vs actual, driver, confidence), 3) Margin
   notes, 4) AR position & data-quality flags, 5) Assumptions & what a human must verify,
   6) Recommended next step. Label every unconfirmed number as "estimate pending review."
   ```

8. **Where the answer lands depends on your version of Word.** Some builds draft straight
   into the document — if so, review it and click **Keep it** (or **Regenerate** /
   **Discard**). If the answer stays in the Copilot pane instead, click **Insert** under the
   response, or **Copy** and paste it into the page. Either way, nothing is final until you
   accept it. Then tighten the Bottom Line so it leads with the single decision Nora must
   make.
9. **Stay in the same Word Copilot pane** and run one revision pass — content creation is
   editing, not generating:

   ```
   Rewrite this memo to be 30% shorter without dropping any figure or caveat. Cut hedging
   language, keep every "estimate pending review" label, and flag any sentence where the
   claim is stronger than the evidence behind it.
   ```

10. **Save the memo** — Tasks 2 and 3 both read from it.

### Task 2 — Build the one-slide leadership summary

A page compressed to a slide. Watch the labels: a bullet has no room for a caveat, so
"estimate pending review" either survives as its own line or quietly becomes a fact in front
of the board.

1. Open **PowerPoint** — press **Start** and type *PowerPoint*, or use the app launcher (the
   grid icon, top-left) in Microsoft 365 on the web. Choose **Create Blank Presentation**,
   then **Create a copy → Create a copy online** → your **`Acme - Finance Month-End`** folder
   → name it **`Month-End Leadership Slide`**.
2. On the **Home** tab, click the **Copilot** button (lower right corner or right of the
   ribbon). In the prompt box type **`/`** and pick **Month-End CFO Memo**, then add:

   ```
   Create ONE executive summary slide from the referenced memo. Title = the headline
   decision. Include: 3 bullets max (biggest driver, margin note, top AR risk), and a
   one-line "what we need from you." Keep it plain and board-ready.
   ```

3. Check that nothing on the slide is an unverified figure presented as fact — if it is,
   label it as an estimate.
4. **Stay in the same PowerPoint Copilot pane** and add the speaker notes you'd actually
   need in the room:

   ```
   Write speaker notes for this slide: 60 seconds of narration, plus the three questions
   the board is most likely to ask and the honest answer to each — including "we don't
   know yet" where that's the truth.
   ```

### Task 3 — Repurpose it for the regional GMs

Same facts, different audience. This is the content-creation muscle worth building.

1. **Go back to your `Month-End CFO Memo` document** and open the **Copilot** pane. The
   memo is right there, so Copilot can read it where it sits:

   ```
   Rewrite the memo in this document as a short email to the four regional GMs. Keep every
   figure identical to the memo — do not add, drop, or round anything. Change only the
   framing: lead with what each region needs to do, keep it under 200 words, plain and
   direct, no finance jargon. End with the deadline and who to reply to.
   ```

2. **Stay in the same Copilot pane** and check for drift — the failure mode in repurposed
   content:

   ```
   Compare the email to the memo. List any number, date, or claim that differs between
   them, including anything rounded or dropped. If they match exactly, say so.
   ```

3. Fix any drift by hand. A figure that changes between two documents is the fastest way
   to lose credibility with a CFO.

### Task 4 — Save your prompt library

This month's memo is this month's. The prompts — and the thresholds inside them — are what
make next month's close faster.

1. Create a new **Word** doc titled **"Acme Finance — Prompt Library"**.
2. Paste in the eight prompts from the
   [Prompt Library](#prompt-library-your-leave-behind) section below.
3. Save it somewhere you'll actually find it again.

This is your leave-behind. Swap in next month's file and the same controls apply.

### Validation checkpoint

- **Source:** Did the memo, slide, and email use only your analysis and AR summary, or did

- **Assumptions:** Is every estimate labeled "estimate pending review" in all three?
- **Risk:** What's the cost if an unverified figure lands in front of the CFO as fact — or
  if the GM email states a number the memo doesn't?
- **Human check:** Who signs off before Nora and the GMs see any of it?

## Prompt Library (your leave-behind)

Save these eight. Anything in `[BRACKETS]` is a blank you fill in — delete the brackets too.
If you're stuck on one, use what you did in this lab: `[SOURCE FILE]` →
*AcmeBottling_Finance_Monthly_Actuals.xlsx*, `[VARIANCE THRESHOLD]` → *+/-15%*,
`[MARGIN THRESHOLD]` → *8 points*, `[AUDIENCE]` → *our CFO*. Where it says **PASTE**, paste
the actual text in.

**1. Variance / anomaly analysis**
```
Using ONLY [SOURCE FILE], analyze budget vs actual. Give the largest unfavorable and
favorable variances (with region/line/%), flag variances beyond [VARIANCE THRESHOLD — e.g.
+/-15%] and margin drops beyond [MARGIN THRESHOLD — e.g. 8 points] and any blanks, and
suggest one verification question per flag. End with assumptions and what you could not
verify.
```
*Set the thresholds yourself. If Copilot decides what counts as an anomaly, this month's run
and next month's aren't comparable — and that's the whole value of a saved prompt.*

**2. Vendor risk vs. financial exposure**
```
Attached: [FINANCIAL RESULTS FILE] and [VENDOR/SUPPLIER FILE]. Map each vendor to the
[THING THEY AFFECT — e.g. product line] it supplies, then cross that against the variance
and margin findings. Return a table: [DIMENSION], budget vs actual, margin move, the vendor
behind it, that vendor's status and score, and a Low/Med/High exposure rating with one line
of reasoning. List separately anything with no vendor match and any vendor matching nothing
— do not guess a mapping. Then state plainly what this data cannot tell me about vendor
risk.
```
*The last sentence is the one that makes this finance-grade. A status field is not an
exposure — without spend, terms, and AP aging, any rating is a hypothesis. Make Copilot say
which of those it's missing, and keep the commercial decision with procurement.*

**3. Spreadsheet QA**
```
Review [SOURCE FILE] for data-quality issues ONLY: invalid dates, mismatched buckets,
negative/zero/blank values, status typos, and possible duplicates. Return row, field,
issue, and suggested fix. Do not change the data.
```
*"Do not change the data" is the control. Copilot proposes; a named person applies.*

**4. Scrubbing rulebook**
```
For the fixes I accepted below, give the exact formula or step to apply each one on
[TAB NAME], flag rather than delete anything questionable in a "QA Flag" column, and do
not delete rows. Then write a rulebook: rule, trigger condition, correction, and whether
it is automatic or needs human judgment. State which rules must never be automated.
Accepted fixes: [PASTE ACCEPTED FIXES HERE]
```
*The rulebook is the reusable asset, not the cleaned file. A clean number nobody can explain
is worse than a dirty one.*

**5. Reporting summary**
```
Using ONLY [SCRUBBED SOURCE], build a summary of [MEASURE — e.g. outstanding balance] by
[DIMENSION — e.g. aging bucket] plus the top [N] by [MEASURE]. Exclude flagged rows and
state exactly how much you excluded and why. Enrich with [REFERENCE DATA] to roll up by
[REGION/OWNER]; list unmatched IDs separately rather than guessing. Show the formulas you
used.
```
*Two clauses turn a summary into a report: the stated exclusion, and unmatched IDs listed
rather than absorbed. Unmatched rows are a finding, not a rounding error.*

**6. Finance executive memo**
```
Draft a one-page finance memo for [AUDIENCE] from the findings below. Structure: Bottom
line, Variance highlights (table), Margin notes, AR position/data-quality, Assumptions &
what to verify, Recommended next step. Label unconfirmed numbers as "estimate pending
review." Findings: [PASTE FINDINGS HERE]
```
*Or, if you saved the findings as a file, reference it with `/` in Word and drop the last
line. That's the version that scales.*

**7. Executive briefing slide**
```
Create ONE board-ready slide from the memo below: title = the decision, [N] bullets max,
one "what we need from you" line. No unverified figure stated as fact.
Memo: [PASTE MEMO HERE]
```
*Then check the labels survived. Compression drops caveats before it drops numbers.*

**8. Repurpose without drift**
```
Rewrite [SOURCE CONTENT] for [NEW AUDIENCE] in [FORMAT, LENGTH]. Keep every figure
identical — do not add, drop, or round anything. Change only framing and tone. Then list
any number, date, or claim that differs from the original; if none, say so.
```
*Never split this one in two. The drift check is the prompt — the rewrite is the easy part,
and it's where Copilot quietly rounds a figure while "just changing the tone."*
