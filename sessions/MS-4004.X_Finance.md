# MS-4004.X — Finance Hands-On Lab
### Acme Bottling · Microsoft 365 Copilot

**Audience:** Finance team members — analysts, accountants, FP&A, and finance managers — who already use Copilot and want to move faster from raw numbers to a reviewed story.

**Format:** ~65–80 minutes of hands-on, inside a ~90-minute session.

**Level:** Intermediate. We skip the basics and get straight into real Acme Bottling work.

Month-end just closed. You'll take an actuals export and find what moved and what looks wrong, scrub a messy AR aging sheet into a reporting-ready summary, and turn the result into a memo, a slide, and a regional email your CFO can act on.

> **How to use this lab:** Just read and follow along. Nothing to install, no repo to download, no code to save. Open the files linked below and go.

---

## What you'll be able to do

- Analyze budget vs actual to find variance drivers, anomalies, and missing values.
- Scrub a messy spreadsheet — bad dates, wrong buckets, negative or blank amounts — into a
  documented, corrected dataset instead of just a list of complaints.
- Turn the scrubbed data into a reporting summary: enriched with customer reference data,
  rolled up by region, and readable at a glance.
- Create finance content for three different audiences from one set of numbers — a CFO
  memo, a board slide, and a regional GM email — without changing the facts.
- Build reusable prompts for variance analysis, scrubbing, reporting, and content.
- Keep a validation habit so no unverified number reaches leadership as fact.

## Learning arc

This lab moves in one direction: **trust the numbers, then tell the story.**

|  | **Exercise 1 — From actuals to a reviewed story** | **Exercise 2 — Scrub the data, then report it** | **Exercise 3 — Turn the numbers into content** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **You're doing** | Finding drivers and anomalies | Cleaning and summarizing AR data | Writing for three audiences |
| **Copilot's job** | Analyze, flag, question its own output | Propose fixes, build the rollup | Draft to a structure, label estimates |
| **Your job** | Decide which flags are real | Decide which fixes get applied | Decide which numbers are safe to state |
| **Stakes** | Internal — only you see it | Internal — but the report feeds everything after | External — the CFO and regional GMs see it |
| **You leave with** | A variance and anomaly list you can defend | A cleaned sheet, a scrubbing rulebook, and an AR summary | A memo, a slide, an email, and a 7-prompt library |

Every task runs the same beat: **prompt → read it critically → keep what's reusable.**
Every exercise ends the same way: **Source / Assumptions / Risk / Human check.**

## The one rule to protect

**Copilot assists. A human validates.** In Finance the control matters as much as the
answer. Every figure Copilot produces is an estimate until a person confirms it — so
every exercise ends with source, assumptions, risk, and who signs off.

# Exercise 1 — From monthly actuals to a reviewed story

**Objective:** Produce a variance and anomaly list you could defend in a review — with the
arithmetic spot-checked and every unverifiable figure labeled.

**Scenario:** Month-end close just finished. Your CFO, **Nora Whitfield**, wants the August
variance story before tomorrow's leadership sync — what moved, why, and what needs a
decision. You have the actuals export; you don't yet trust every number in it.

### Task 1 — Set your finance voice and get oriented in Excel

First you'll tell Copilot how to behave with finance data, then get the shape of the file.

1. Open **Copilot Chat** and go to **Settings** (gear icon or your profile picture, top
   right) → **Personalization**.
2. Add this **custom instruction** and save:

   ```
   When I work with finance data: be precise and controls-first. Lead with the headline
   number and the biggest driver. Always separate confirmed facts from estimates, label
   anything unverified as "estimate pending review," and end with the assumptions you
   made and what a human should verify. Use plain English, no jargon.
   ```

   *No Personalization option in your tenant? Paste this block at the top of your first
   prompt in each exercise instead.* This one step changes the tone of everything below —
   you'll see Copilot flag its own assumptions without being asked each time.
3. Open `AcmeBottling_Finance_Monthly_Actuals.xlsx` in **Excel**. Note the two tabs:
   *Aug 2026 Actuals* and *YTD by Region*.
4. Click anywhere in the data and press **Ctrl+T** to format it as a table (accept the
   defaults). Copilot in Excel works much better on a real table.
5. On the **Home** tab, click the **Copilot** button, then paste:

   ```
   Give me a quick overview of this actuals table: columns, number of rows, total budget
   vs total actual revenue, and the overall variance %. Then list any rows where the
   Actual Revenue is blank or the Notes column is filled in.
   ```

6. Jot down what Copilot flags — you'll dig into those next.

> **Why this matters:** You're establishing the shape of the data before you ask it for
> conclusions. Skip this and you can't tell a real anomaly from a formatting artifact.

### Task 2 — Use Analyst to find variance drivers and anomalies

1. Open **Copilot Chat**, then open **Agents** in the left sidebar (or the agent picker
   above the prompt box) and select **Analyst**.
2. Attach `AcmeBottling_Finance_Monthly_Actuals.xlsx` using the paperclip icon.
3. Paste this analysis prompt — note it **defines what counts as an exception** so results
   are repeatable:

   ```
   Using ONLY the attached actuals file, analyze budget vs actual. Do three things:
   1) Identify the 3 largest unfavorable revenue variances and the 2 largest favorable
      ones, with region, product line, and variance %.
   2) Flag any anomalies: variances at or beyond +/-15%, gross-margin drops of 8 points
      or more, and any blank/missing actuals.
   3) For each flagged item, suggest one question I should ask before I trust it.
   End with the assumptions you made and which numbers you could not verify.
   ```

> **Model note:** if the reasoning looks shallow or the math seems off, pick a **more
> capable model** in the model picker near the prompt box and re-run. Finance analysis is a
> good case for the stronger reasoning model.

### Task 3 — Spot-check the flags before you trust them

1. Go back to the spreadsheet and verify **two** flagged rows by hand. Confirm the
   missing-actual row and the large Midwest miss both show up.
2. Then make Copilot show its work — paste into the same Analyst chat:

   ```
   For the three unfavorable variances you flagged, show the exact budget figure, actual
   figure, and the arithmetic you used for each variance %. If any figure came from a
   blank or inferred value, say so explicitly.
   ```

3. If the arithmetic doesn't reconcile to the sheet, that's your finding — not Copilot's.

### Validation checkpoint

In a scratch doc, answer for this analysis:

- **Source:** Which file/tab did Copilot use? Any row it couldn't read?
- **Assumptions:** How did it treat the blank actual? Did it annualize or assume anything?
- **Risk:** What's the cost if a flagged variance is wrong — or a real one is missed?
- **Human check:** Who confirms these numbers before they reach the CFO?

> **Exercise 1 output:** a variance and anomaly list with the arithmetic spot-checked and
> every unverifiable figure labeled.

# Exercise 2 — Scrub the data, then report it

**Objective:** Catch the planted data-quality problems, decide and apply the fixes on your
terms, and turn the corrected sheet into an AR summary that's safe to report from.

**Scenario:** Nora won't look at an AR number until someone can say how it was cleaned. You
have a messy aging sheet and a customer reference list. You need a corrected dataset, a
written record of what you changed, and a regional rollup you can hand to anyone.

### Task 1 — Find the problems (propose, don't change)

1. Open `AcmeBottling_Finance_AR_Aging.xlsx` in **Excel** and go to the *AR Aging* tab.
2. Click anywhere in the data and press **Ctrl+T** to format it as a table.
3. On the **Home** tab, click the **Copilot** button, then paste:

   ```
   Review this AR aging sheet for data-quality problems ONLY within this file. Check for:
   inconsistent or invalid date formats, aging buckets that don't match Days Past Due,
   negative or zero amounts, blank Days Past Due, status typos, and possible duplicate
   invoices. Return a table: row, field, the issue, and the suggested fix. Do not change
   the data — just list the problems.
   ```

4. Confirm Copilot catches the big ones: the bad date format, the mis-bucketed 90+ invoice,
   the negative amount, the blank/negative days-past-due, and the status typo.

> **Why:** "Do not change the data" is the control. Copilot proposes; you decide what gets
> corrected and by whom.

### Task 2 — Scrub it: apply the fixes and write the rulebook

Now you turn that list into a *cleaned* dataset — and, more importantly, into rules you
can rerun next month.

1. Copy the *AR Aging* tab to a new tab named **AR Aging (Scrubbed)**. Never scrub in
   place; you want the original to reconcile back to.
2. Decide which of Copilot's proposed fixes you accept. Mark any you reject and why.
3. In the Copilot pane on the scrubbed tab, paste:

   ```
   Work only on this tab. For each fix I accepted below, give me the exact Excel formula
   or step to apply it — normalizing all Invoice Dates to a single format, recalculating
   the Aging Bucket from Days Past Due, and flagging (do not delete) negative amounts,
   blanks, and suspected duplicates in a new "QA Flag" column. Do not delete any row.
   Accepted fixes: [PASTE].
   ```

4. Apply the steps yourself and confirm the row count is unchanged and the total AR
   balance still ties to the original tab (any difference must be explainable).
5. Ask Copilot to document what you just did:

   ```
   Write a short scrubbing rulebook for this sheet: each rule, the condition that triggers
   it, the correction applied, and whether it is automatic or needs human judgment. Add a
   final line stating which rules must never be automated.
   ```

   Keep that rulebook — it's the reusable asset, not the cleaned file.

> **Why this matters:** A clean number nobody can explain is worse than a dirty one.
> The rulebook is what makes next month's close faster and auditable.

### Task 3 — Build the AR reporting summary

1. Open `AcmeBottling_Customers.csv` alongside the workbook. It carries region, segment,
   account manager, and payment terms for each `CustomerID`.
2. In the Copilot pane, on the scrubbed tab, paste:

   ```
   Using ONLY the scrubbed tab, build an AR reporting summary on a new tab: total
   outstanding and invoice count by aging bucket, and the top 5 customers by 90+ balance.
   Exclude any row where QA Flag is set, and state exactly how much balance you excluded
   and why. Show the formulas you used.
   ```

3. Enrich it. Paste in the relevant `AcmeBottling_Customers.csv` rows (or add them as a
   reference tab) and ask:

   ```
   Add region and account manager to the summary using the customer reference data I
   pasted. Roll up 90+ balance by region and by account manager. If a CustomerID has no
   match in the reference data, list it separately as unmatched — do not guess.
   ```

4. Ask for one chart: *"Add a simple column chart of 90+ balance by region, titled for a
   finance audience."*
5. Spot-check one region total by hand against the scrubbed rows. If it doesn't tie, that's
   your finding.

> **Why:** "Do not guess" plus a stated exclusion amount is what turns a summary into a
> report. Unmatched rows are a finding, not a rounding error.

### Validation checkpoint

- **Source:** Which tab did each number come from — original, scrubbed, or reference data?
- **Assumptions:** What did the scrub change, and what balance did the report exclude?
- **Risk:** What breaks downstream if a bucket was recalculated wrong?
- **Human check:** Who approves the scrubbing rulebook before it's reused?

> **Exercise 2 output:** a scrubbed AR tab, a scrubbing rulebook, and a regional AR summary
> with a chart and a stated exclusion.

# Exercise 3 — Turn the numbers into content

**Objective:** Create three pieces of finance content from one set of verified numbers —
a CFO memo, a board slide, and a regional GM email — with no unverified figure stated as
fact and no facts drifting between versions.

**Scenario:** The analysis is done and the AR data is clean. Nora needs a memo, the board
needs one slide, and the regional GMs need an email that lands differently without saying
anything different.

### Task 1 — Draft the finance memo in Word

1. Open `AcmeBottling_Leadership_Audience_Profile.docx` and
   `AcmeBottling_Executive_Memo_Format.docx` — read who you're writing for and the
   structure to match.
2. Open **Word**, create a new document, and on the **Home** tab click the **Copilot**
   button.
3. Paste this prompt, replacing the `[PASTE]` placeholders with the audience profile text,
   your Exercise 1 findings, and your Exercise 2 AR summary:

   ```
   Draft a one-page finance executive memo for the audience described below, using my
   variance findings and AR summary. Follow this structure exactly: 1) Bottom line
   (2–3 sentences), 2) Variance highlights (table: area, budget vs actual, driver,
   confidence), 3) Margin notes, 4) AR position & data-quality flags, 5) Assumptions &
   what a human must verify, 6) Recommended next step. Label every unconfirmed number as
   "estimate pending review." Audience: [PASTE FROM PROFILE]. Findings: [PASTE].
   AR summary: [PASTE].
   ```

4. Tighten the Bottom Line so it leads with the single decision Nora must make.
5. Run one revision pass on the draft — content creation is editing, not generating:

   ```
   Rewrite this memo to be 30% shorter without dropping any figure or caveat. Cut hedging
   language, keep every "estimate pending review" label, and flag any sentence where the
   claim is stronger than the evidence behind it.
   ```

### Task 2 — Build the one-slide leadership summary

1. Open **PowerPoint** and create a new blank presentation.
2. On the **Home** tab, click the **Copilot** button, then paste — replacing `[PASTE]`
   with your memo text:

   ```
   Create ONE executive summary slide from the memo I paste below. Title = the headline
   decision. Include: 3 bullets max (biggest driver, margin note, top AR risk), and a
   one-line "what we need from you." Keep it plain and board-ready. Memo: [PASTE]
   ```

3. Check that nothing on the slide is an unverified figure presented as fact — if it is,
   label it as an estimate.
4. Add the speaker notes you'd actually need in the room:

   ```
   Write speaker notes for this slide: 60 seconds of narration, plus the three questions
   the board is most likely to ask and the honest answer to each — including "we don't
   know yet" where that's the truth.
   ```

### Task 3 — Repurpose it for the regional GMs

Same facts, different audience. This is the content-creation muscle worth building.

1. Back in **Word** (or Copilot Chat), paste your memo and this prompt:

   ```
   Rewrite this memo as a short email to the four regional GMs. Keep every figure
   identical to the memo — do not add, drop, or round anything. Change only the framing:
   lead with what each region needs to do, keep it under 200 words, plain and direct, no
   finance jargon. End with the deadline and who to reply to. Memo: [PASTE]
   ```

2. Now check for drift — the failure mode in repurposed content:

   ```
   Compare the email to the memo. List any number, date, or claim that differs between
   them, including anything rounded or dropped. If they match exactly, say so.
   ```

3. Fix any drift by hand. A figure that changes between two documents is the fastest way
   to lose credibility with a CFO.

### Task 4 — Save your prompt library

1. Create a new **Word** doc titled **"Acme Finance — Prompt Library"**.
2. Paste in the seven prompts from the
   [Prompt Library](#prompt-library-your-leave-behind) section below.
3. Save it somewhere you'll actually find it again.

This is your leave-behind. Swap in next month's file and the same controls apply.

### Validation checkpoint

- **Source:** Did the memo, slide, and email use only your analysis and AR summary, or did
  Copilot reach outside them?
- **Assumptions:** Is every estimate labeled "estimate pending review" in all three?
- **Risk:** What's the cost if an unverified figure lands in front of the CFO as fact — or
  if the GM email states a number the memo doesn't?
- **Human check:** Who signs off before Nora and the GMs see any of it?

> **Exercise 3 output:** a one-page memo, a leadership slide with speaker notes, a regional
> GM email that matches the memo figure-for-figure, and a 7-prompt library.

## Prompt Library (your leave-behind)

Save these seven. Fill the `[placeholders]` each time.

**1. Variance / anomaly analysis**
```
Using ONLY [SOURCE FILE], analyze budget vs actual. Give the largest unfavorable and
favorable variances (with region/line/%), flag variances beyond [VARIANCE THRESHOLD] and
margin drops beyond [MARGIN THRESHOLD] and any blanks, and suggest one verification
question per flag. End with assumptions and what you could not verify.
```

**2. Spreadsheet QA**
```
Review [SOURCE FILE] for data-quality issues ONLY: invalid dates, mismatched buckets,
negative/zero/blank values, status typos, and possible duplicates. Return row, field,
issue, and suggested fix. Do not change the data.
```

**3. Scrubbing rulebook**
```
For the fixes I accepted below, give the exact formula or step to apply each one on
[TAB NAME], flag rather than delete anything questionable in a "QA Flag" column, and do
not delete rows. Then write a rulebook: rule, trigger condition, correction, and whether
it is automatic or needs human judgment. State which rules must never be automated.
Accepted fixes: [PASTE]
```

**4. Reporting summary**
```
Using ONLY [SCRUBBED SOURCE], build a summary of [MEASURE] by [DIMENSION] plus the top
[N] by [MEASURE]. Exclude flagged rows and state exactly how much you excluded and why.
Enrich with [REFERENCE DATA] to roll up by [REGION/OWNER]; list unmatched IDs separately
rather than guessing. Show the formulas you used.
```

**5. Finance executive memo**
```
Draft a one-page finance memo for [AUDIENCE] from the findings below. Structure: Bottom
line, Variance highlights (table), Margin notes, AR position/data-quality, Assumptions &
what to verify, Recommended next step. Label unconfirmed numbers as "estimate pending
review." Findings: [PASTE]
```

**6. Executive briefing slide**
```
Create ONE board-ready slide from the memo below: title = the decision, 3 bullets max,
one "what we need from you" line. No unverified figure stated as fact. Memo: [PASTE]
```

**7. Repurpose without drift**
```
Rewrite [SOURCE CONTENT] for [NEW AUDIENCE] in [FORMAT, LENGTH]. Keep every figure
identical — do not add, drop, or round anything. Change only framing and tone. Then list
any number, date, or claim that differs from the original; if none, say so.
```

## Facilitator notes

- **Data hygiene:** Ex.2 Task 1's "do not change the data" instruction is the teachable
  habit — Copilot proposes corrections, a person applies them. Ex.2 Task 2 is where it
  pays off: the scrubbing rulebook, not the cleaned file, is the reusable asset.
- **Reporting control:** In Ex.2 Task 3, watch for groups that accept a summary without
  checking the excluded balance or the unmatched CustomerIDs. That's the whole lesson — a
  report is only trustworthy if it declares what it left out.
- **Content creation:** Ex.3 Task 3 (the GM email) is the one to protect if time is short
  — the drift check is where participants see Copilot quietly round or drop a figure while
  "just changing the tone."
- **Finance reality:** Reinforce that Copilot does not replace financial judgment. The
  Ex.1 Task 3 show-your-arithmetic step is the moment this lands — if the math doesn't
  reconcile to the sheet, the participant just caught it, and that's the whole point.
- **Capture agent ideas:** If someone says "I want an agent that reconciles the ledger" or
  "auto-close," that's a real agent opportunity — capture it and route to **Chris
  Bartlow**. Keep this lab current-state Copilot.
- **Timing:** Roughly 20 / 30 / 25 minutes across the three exercises. If you're tight,
  cut Ex.3 Task 2 step 4 (speaker notes) — never the drift check. Fast groups can rerun
  the Ex.1 analysis with the threshold changed to +/-10% to prove the prompt is genuinely
  reusable, or bring in the YTD tab.
