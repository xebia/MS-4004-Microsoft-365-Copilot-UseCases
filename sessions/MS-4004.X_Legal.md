# MS-4004.X — Legal Hands-On Lab
### Acme Bottling · Microsoft 365 Copilot

**Session:** 7 of 7 · Legal
**Audience:** Legal team members who already use Copilot and want to move faster on
contract review, clause comparison, and executive briefings.
**Format:** ~60 minutes of hands-on (up to ~75 if your group goes deep), inside a
~90-minute session.
**Level:** Intermediate. We skip the basics and get straight into real Acme Bottling work.

You'll take a customer contract and Acme's standard-language playbook, summarize with
citations, compare clause by clause, flag what's missing or non-standard, and produce an
executive briefing.

> **How to use this lab:** Just read and follow along. Nothing to install, no repo to
> download, no code to save. Open the files linked below and go.

---

## What you'll be able to do

- Summarize a contract clause by clause and cite the source clause numbers.
- Compare a customer contract against Acme's standard language.
- Flag missing, weaker, or non-standard terms and the risk each carries.
- Produce an executive legal briefing with clear recommendations.
- Build reusable prompts for summaries, clause comparison, briefings, and talking points.

## Learning arc

This lab moves in one direction: **find the truth, then act on it.**

|  | **Exercise 1 — Summarize and compare** | **Exercise 2 — Brief the decision-makers** |
|---|---|---|
| **You're doing** | Reading a contract against a playbook | Turning gaps into a negotiating position |
| **Copilot's job** | Describe and compare, with citations | Draft to a structure, mark what needs sign-off |
| **Your job** | Decide what's actually written vs. inferred | Decide what a non-lawyer can safely act on |
| **Stakes** | Internal — only you see it | External — leadership negotiates from it |
| **You leave with** | A cited, risk-rated gap list | A briefing, talking points, and a 4-prompt library |

Every task runs the same beat: **prompt → read it critically → keep what's reusable.**
Every exercise ends the same way: **Source / Assumptions / Risk / Human check.**

# Exercise 1 — Summarize and compare (about 30 min)

**Scenario:** A supply agreement came in from Northwind Distilling. You need to know what
it says and where it drifts from Acme's standard terms.

### Task 1 — Set your legal voice and clean your context (8 min)

1. First, tune Copilot for this work. In **Copilot Chat** settings, add a **custom
   instruction**:

   ```
   When I review contracts: describe only what the document actually says and cite the
   clause number every time. Never state a legal conclusion or give advice — flag issues
   for attorney review instead. If a term is absent, say it is absent rather than
   inferring what was intended.
   ```

2. In OneDrive, create a folder named **`Acme - Northwind Contract Review`**.
3. Save both the customer contract and the standard-terms playbook into it.
4. Work from that folder / a fresh chat so Copilot references only these two documents.

> **Why:** One folder, one clean chat per matter keeps the comparison scoped. The custom
> instruction is what keeps a drafting tool from sounding like counsel.

### Task 2 — Summarize the contract with Researcher (10 min)

1. Open **Copilot Chat**, turn on **Researcher**, attach
   `AcmeBottling_Customer_Contract_Sample.md`, and paste:

   ```
   Summarize this customer supply agreement clause by clause: payment, delivery,
   liability, warranty, force majeure, termination, confidentiality, governing law, and
   any extra clauses. For each, give a one-line plain-English description and cite the
   clause number. Do not add legal opinions — just describe what's there.
   ```

2. You now have a clean, cited map of the contract.
3. Spot-check two clauses against the document. If Copilot cited a clause number that
   doesn't match the text, that's your finding — and a reason to distrust the rest.

### Task 3 — Compare against Acme's standard language (6 min)

1. Attach `AcmeBottling_Standard_Terms.md` to the same chat and paste:

   ```
   Compare this customer contract against Acme's standard terms playbook, clause by
   clause. For each clause, say whether it Matches, is Weaker/Non-standard, or is Missing,
   and note the specific difference (e.g., Net 45 vs. standard Net 30, no liability cap,
   30-day vs. 90-day warranty, added exclusivity clause). Flag the risk level (Low/Med/
   High) for each gap. Cite clause numbers. Do not give a legal conclusion — flag for
   human review.
   ```

2. Review the comparison. Note the high-risk gaps: the blank liability cap and the added
   exclusivity clause.

### Validation checkpoint (6 min)

- **Source:** Did Copilot use only the contract and the standard-terms playbook?
- **Assumptions:** Did it infer any term that isn't actually written in the contract?
- **Risk:** What's the exposure if the missing liability cap slips through?
- **Human check:** Which clauses will a lawyer review before anything goes back to the customer?

> **Exercise 1 output:** a cited clause-by-clause map and a risk-rated gap list against
> Acme's playbook.

# Exercise 2 — Brief the decision-makers (about 30 min)

**Scenario:** Leadership needs a short, clear read on this contract before the next
negotiation call.

### Task 1 — Draft the executive legal briefing in Word (10 min)

1. Open a new **Word** doc in the review folder.
2. Open **Copilot in Word** and paste:

   ```
   Write a one-page executive legal briefing on the Northwind Distilling contract review.
   Structure it as: Overview, Key gaps vs. Acme standard terms (with risk levels), The
   three issues to negotiate first, and Recommended next steps. Keep it under 300 words,
   plain English for a non-lawyer audience. End with a "For legal review before action"
   line listing the clauses that need sign-off.
   ```

3. Review. Confirm the "For legal review before action" line is present.

### Task 2 — Draft negotiation talking points (8 min)

1. In the same doc or a new one, open **Copilot** and paste:

   ```
   From the briefing, write short negotiation talking points for the three highest-risk
   items (missing liability cap, added exclusivity clause, Net 45 payment terms). For each,
   give our position in one or two sentences and a fallback. Practical and direct, no legal
   jargon. Mark each as "draft — attorney to confirm."
   ```

2. Read for clarity. Confirm each point is marked for attorney sign-off.

### Task 3 — Check for legal conclusions that slipped in (6 min)

1. Paste the briefing and talking points back into the clean chat:

   ```
   Review the text below. Flag any sentence that states a legal conclusion, gives advice,
   or asserts what a clause "means" rather than what it says. For each, cite the clause it
   refers to and rewrite it as a neutral description plus a review flag. Text: [PASTE]
   ```

2. Rewrite anything flagged. **This is a first pass, not an approval** — an attorney still
   owns the final read.

### Task 4 — Save your prompt library (3 min)

Create a Word doc titled **"Acme Legal — Prompt Library"** and paste in the four prompts
from the [Prompt Library](#prompt-library-your-leave-behind) section below.

This is your leave-behind. Swap in the next contract and the same playbook, and reuse.

### Validation checkpoint (3 min)

- **Source:** Did the briefing stay grounded in the contract and the standard-terms playbook?
- **Assumptions:** Did Copilot state a legal conclusion it shouldn't have?
- **Risk:** What's the cost of briefing leadership on an inaccurate clause read?
- **Human check:** Which attorney signs off before this informs the negotiation?

> **Exercise 2 output:** a one-page executive briefing, negotiation talking points marked
> for attorney sign-off, and a 4-prompt library.

## Facilitator notes

- **Data hygiene:** The clean-folder step (Ex.1 Task 1) is the teachable habit — one
  matter, one folder, one clean chat. Reinforce the sanitized-sample rule; Legal is a
  session where someone may be tempted to paste a live agreement.
- **Context workaround:** Reinforce that citing the standard-terms playbook in each prompt
  is the current-state substitute for a persistent clause library.
- **Legal reality:** The teachable moment is Ex.2 Task 3. Copilot drifts from "the clause
  says X" to "this means we're exposed" without being asked. Catching that drift is the
  difference between a drafting aid and unlicensed advice.
- **Capture agent ideas:** If someone imagines "a clause-library agent that auto-compares
  every incoming contract," that's a real agent opportunity — capture it and route to
  **Chris Bartlow**. Keep this lab current-state Copilot.
- **Timing:** Fast groups can run the comparison on a second sanitized contract against the
  same playbook.
