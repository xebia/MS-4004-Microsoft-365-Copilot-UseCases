# MS-4004.X — Legal Hands-On Lab
### Acme Bottling · Microsoft 365 Copilot

**Audience:** Legal team members who already use Copilot and want to move faster on contract review, clause comparison, and executive briefings.

**Format:** ~65 minutes of hands-on

**Level:** Intermediate. We skip the basics and get straight into real Acme Bottling work.

You'll take a customer contract and Acme's standard-language playbook, summarize with citations, compare clause by clause, flag what's missing or non-standard, and produce an executive briefing.

> **How to use this lab:** Just read and follow along. Nothing to install, no repo to download, no code to save. Open the files linked below and go.

---

## What you'll be able to do

- Summarize a contract clause by clause and cite the source clause numbers.
- Compare a customer contract against Acme's standard language.
- Flag missing, weaker, or non-standard terms and the risk each carries.
- Research commercial norms with citations, and keep that research labeled as background
  rather than letting it become advice about your agreement.
- Produce an executive legal briefing with clear recommendations.
- Build reusable prompts for summaries, clause comparison, research, briefings, and talking
  points.
- Keep a validation habit so no legal conclusion reaches leadership without an attorney behind it.

## Learning arc

This lab moves in one direction: **find the truth, then act on it.**

|  | **Exercise 1 — Summarize and compare** | **Exercise 2 — Brief the decision-makers** |
|---|---|---|
| **You're doing** | Reading a contract against a playbook | Turning gaps into a negotiating position |
| **Copilot's job** | Describe and compare, with citations | Research the norms, draft to a structure, mark what needs sign-off |
| **Your job** | Decide what's actually written vs. inferred | Decide what a non-lawyer can safely act on |
| **Stakes** | Internal — only you see it | External — leadership negotiates from it |
| **You leave with** | A cited, risk-rated gap list | Cited market background, a briefing, talking points, and a 5-prompt library |

Every task runs the same beat: **prompt → read it critically → keep what's reusable.**
Every exercise ends the same way: **Source / Assumptions / Risk / Human check.**

## What this means before a clause is signed

Copilot flags issues — it does not give legal conclusions. A clause Copilot missed and one
it summarized confidently look the same on screen, so every point traces back to a cited
clause. Every exercise ends with source, assumptions, risk, and what a lawyer must review.

# Exercise 1 — Summarize and compare

**Objective:** Produce a cited, clause-by-clause map of the contract and a risk-rated list
of every gap against Acme's standard terms.

**Scenario:** A supply agreement came in from Northwind Distilling. You need to know what
it says and where it drifts from Acme's standard terms.

### Task 1 — Set your legal voice and clean your context

First you'll tell Copilot to describe rather than advise, then scope it to this matter.

1. Open **Copilot Chat** and go to **Settings** (gear icon or your profile picture, top
   right) → **Personalization**.
2. Add this **custom instruction** and save:

   ```
   When I review contracts: describe only what the document actually says and cite the
   clause number every time. Never state a legal conclusion or give advice — flag issues
   for attorney review instead. If a term is absent, say it is absent rather than
   inferring what was intended.
   ```

   *No Personalization option in your tenant? Paste this block at the top of your first
   prompt in each exercise instead.*
3. In Copilot Chat, start a **New chat** (the pencil or **New chat** icon at the top of
   the pane) so Copilot references only these two documents.

> **Why:** One folder, one clean chat per matter keeps the comparison scoped. The custom
> instruction is what keeps a drafting tool from sounding like counsel.

### Task 2 — Summarize the contract with Researcher

**Researcher** is a specialized agent that runs multi-step research across the web and your
work data, then returns an answer with citations — instead of the single-pass reply a normal
chat prompt gives you. Use it here for the citation discipline, not the reach: a clause map
is only useful if every line points back to a clause number you can check.

1. In Copilot Chat, open **Agents** in the left sidebar (or the agent picker above the
   prompt box) and select **Researcher**.
2. Attach `AcmeBottling_Customer_Contract_Sample.md` using the paperclip icon.
3. Paste this prompt and press Enter:

   ```
   Summarize this customer supply agreement clause by clause: payment, delivery,
   liability, warranty, force majeure, termination, confidentiality, governing law, and
   any extra clauses. For each, give a one-line plain-English description and cite the
   clause number. Do not add legal opinions — just describe what's there.
   ```

4. **Researcher may ask you a question before it runs** — typically whether to bring in
   general contract-law context or benchmark the terms against market standards. Say
   **no**: this task is a description of what the document says, nothing more. Outside
   context is how a summary quietly turns into legal advice. Reply **"go ahead"** only if
   it's asking about format.
5. You now have a clean, cited map of the contract.
6. Spot-check two clauses against the document. If Copilot cited a clause number that
   doesn't match the text, that's your finding — and a reason to distrust the rest.

> **If Researcher isn't available in your tenant,** it's licensed separately from Copilot
> Chat in some setups. Run the same prompt in a normal **Copilot Chat** thread with the
> contract attached — you lose the multi-step reasoning, but the clause map and the citation
> requirement both still work, and those are the parts that matter here.

### Task 3 — Compare against Acme's standard language

This is the task the whole lab is built around. Copilot doesn't hold a persistent clause
library, so you hand it the playbook every time — that's the workaround, and it's why both
files have to sit in the same chat. Note the three-way verdict the prompt demands
(Matches / Weaker / Missing): **Missing** is the one Copilot will skip if you don't name it,
because absent text is the hardest thing for a language model to notice.

1. **Stay in the same chat** and attach `AcmeBottling_Standard_Terms.md` as a second file
   (paperclip icon again — both files stay in context).
2. Paste:

   ```
   Compare this customer contract against Acme's standard terms playbook, clause by
   clause. For each clause, say whether it Matches, is Weaker/Non-standard, or is Missing,
   and note the specific difference (e.g., Net 45 vs. standard Net 30, no liability cap,
   30-day vs. 90-day warranty, added exclusivity clause). Flag the risk level (Low/Med/
   High) for each gap. Cite clause numbers. Do not give a legal conclusion — flag for
   human review.
   ```

3. Review the comparison. Note the high-risk gaps: the blank liability cap and the added
   exclusivity clause.

**Save the comparison so Exercise 2 can read it.** Don't scroll back for the clause map from
Task 2 and the gap list from this one. Make the chat hand you both in a single block.

4. **Stay in the same chat** and paste:

   ```
   Recap this review as one block I can copy: first the clause-by-clause map with its
   citations, then the comparison against Acme's standard terms with the Matches /
   Weaker-Non-standard / Missing verdict, the specific difference, and the risk level for
   each gap. Keep every clause number. Use headings and bullets. No preamble.
   ```

   *If Researcher says it doesn't have the clause map, scroll up and paste that answer back
   under the prompt. It often treats each run as a fresh research task rather than a
   continuation of the last one.*

5. Copy that answer. Open **Word** — press **Start** and type *Word*, or use the app
   launcher (the grid icon, top-left) in Microsoft 365 on the web — choose **Blank
   document** and paste it in.
6. Save it: **Create a copy → Create a copy online** → your
   **`Acme - Northwind Contract Review`** folder → name it **`Northwind Clause Comparison`**.

> **Why this one save matters:** Copilot's context stops at the app boundary — Word can't
> see this chat. A file in your lab folder it can read. From here on you'll point Copilot at
> the comparison instead of re-pasting it, which is the habit worth taking back to work.

### Validation checkpoint

- **Source:** Did Copilot use only the contract and the standard-terms playbook?
- **Assumptions:** Did it infer any term that isn't actually written in the contract?
- **Risk:** What's the exposure if the missing liability cap slips through?
- **Human check:** Which clauses will a lawyer review before anything goes back to the customer?

> **Exercise 1 output:** a cited clause-by-clause map and a risk-rated gap list against
> Acme's playbook.

# Exercise 2 — Brief the decision-makers

**Objective:** Turn the gap list into a one-page briefing a non-lawyer can act on, back the
three hardest terms with cited market research, and produce negotiation talking points with
every legal conclusion caught and neutralized.

**Scenario:** Leadership needs a short, clear read on this contract before the next
negotiation call — and they're going to ask whether Northwind's terms are actually unusual
or just unfamiliar.

### Task 1 — Draft the executive legal briefing in Word

The audience changes everything here: leadership isn't reading this to understand the
contract, they're reading it to decide what to push back on. The `/` reference is how this
pane gets your comparison — it can't see the chat you built it in.

1. Open **Word** — press **Start** and type *Word*, or use the app launcher (the grid icon,
   top-left) in Microsoft 365 on the web. Choose **Blank document**, then **Create a copy →
   Create a copy online** → your **`Acme - Northwind Contract Review`** folder → name it
   **`Northwind Legal Briefing`**.
2. On the **Home** tab, click the **Copilot** button (lower right corner or right of the
   ribbon). In the prompt box type **`/`** and pick **Northwind Clause Comparison**, then
   add:

   ```
   From the referenced comparison, write a one-page executive legal briefing on the
   Northwind Distilling contract review. Structure it as: Overview, Key gaps vs. Acme
   standard terms (with risk levels), The three issues to negotiate first, and Recommended
   next steps. Keep it under 300 words, plain English for a non-lawyer audience. End with a
   "For legal review before action" line listing the clauses that need sign-off.
   ```

3. **Where the answer lands depends on your version of Word.** Some builds draft straight
   into the document — if so, review it and click **Keep it** (or **Regenerate** /
   **Discard**). If the answer stays in the Copilot pane instead, click **Insert** under the
   response, or **Copy** and paste it into the page. Either way, nothing is final until you
   accept it.
4. Review. Confirm the "For legal review before action" line is present, then **save**.

### Task 2 — Research the market position (background, not advice)

In Exercise 1 you told Researcher **no** when it offered outside context — and that was
correct, because you were describing what a document says. The job just changed. Leadership
is about to argue that Northwind's terms are unusual, and *"unusual compared to what?"* is a
research question, not a reading question.

Keep the two apart for the rest of the lab: **outside research shapes your negotiating
position; the contract and the playbook remain the only sources for what the agreement
says.**

1. Start a **New chat** in Copilot Chat and select **Researcher** again. The clean thread
   matters here — your Exercise 1 chat is saturated with *"describe only this document,"*
   and you're about to ask for close to the opposite.
2. Paste this prompt and press Enter. Note that it describes the *category* rather than
   naming Acme or the customer — that keeps the question answerable and keeps your matter
   out of a web search:

   ```
   I'm preparing to negotiate a B2B supply agreement between a U.S. packaging manufacturer
   and a mid-size beverage customer. Research general commercial norms for three terms:
   liability caps, exclusivity commitments, and payment terms. For each, tell me what is
   commonly treated as market-standard, what is considered aggressive, and what tends to be
   traded away in exchange for what. Prioritize recent sources, cite a source for every
   point, and separate what you verified from what you inferred. This is background research
   only — do not give legal advice and do not tell me what any specific contract means.
   ```

3. **Researcher may ask a scoping question first** — jurisdiction, industry, or how recent
   the sources should be. Unlike Exercise 1, here the answer is to engage: narrow it to U.S.
   commercial agreements and recent evidence. Read what it asked, though — those are the
   scoping decisions you should be making *in the prompt* next time.
4. **Open two of the citations and read them.** A market norm you can't trace to a real
   source has no business in a negotiation.
5. Label the output before you use it anywhere. Write one line at the top in your own words:
   *"Market background — not legal advice, not specific to this agreement."*

> **Why:** This is the one step in the lab that reaches outside your tenant, so it's the one
> place Copilot can hand you something nobody approved. Research can tell you that an
> uncapped liability clause is unusual in this category. It cannot tell you whether *this*
> uncapped clause is acceptable — that's an attorney's call, and the label is what keeps the
> line visible to whoever reads your notes next.

> **If Researcher isn't available in your tenant,** run the same prompt in a normal
> **Copilot Chat** thread with web search on. You'll get fewer sources and less depth — so
> lean harder on step 4, and treat anything uncited as unusable.

### Task 3 — Draft negotiation talking points

A briefing describes. Talking points commit. That's a real line to cross, which is why every
point carries a fallback position *and* a "draft — attorney to confirm" mark: the person
using these will be on a call, not at a desk. Bring your market background in here — clearly
labeled — so a fallback is grounded in what the market actually does rather than instinct.

1. In the same Word doc (or a new one), open **Copilot** on the **Home** tab and paste:

   ```
   From the briefing, write short negotiation talking points for the three highest-risk
   items (missing liability cap, added exclusivity clause, Net 45 payment terms). For each,
   give our position in one or two sentences and a fallback. Where market background
   supports a fallback, say so and label it "market background, not advice." Practical and
   direct, no legal jargon. Mark each as "draft — attorney to confirm."
   ```

2. Read for clarity. Confirm each point is marked for attorney sign-off.

### Task 4 — Check for legal conclusions that slipped in

Everything you've written gets checked in one place, before any of it leaves the team.
Copilot drifts from "the clause says X" to "this means we're exposed" without ever being
asked to — and on the page those two sentences look identical. Market research makes the
drift easier, not harder: "most agreements cap liability" is one short step from "we're
exposed," and only one of those is something you're allowed to write. Here you make Copilot
find its own drift.

1. **Stay in the same Word Copilot pane** — the briefing and the talking points are both
   in this document, so Copilot can read them where they sit. Paste:

   ```
   Review the text in this document. Flag any sentence that states a legal conclusion,
   gives advice, asserts what a clause "means" rather than what it says, or presents market
   background as a fact about this agreement. For each, cite the clause it refers to and
   rewrite it as a neutral description plus a review flag.
   ```

2. Rewrite anything flagged. **This is a first pass, not an approval** — an attorney still
   owns the final read.

### Task 5 — Save your prompt library

The Northwind briefing is for Northwind. The prompts are for every contract after it.

1. Create a new **Word** doc titled **"Acme Legal — Prompt Library"**.
2. Paste in the five prompts from the
   [Prompt Library](#prompt-library-your-leave-behind) section below.
3. Save it somewhere you'll actually find it again.

This is your leave-behind. Swap in the next contract and the same playbook, and reuse.

### Validation checkpoint

- **Source:** Did the briefing stay grounded in the contract and the standard-terms
  playbook — and is your market research still clearly separate from both?
- **Assumptions:** Did Copilot state a legal conclusion it shouldn't have, or quietly turn a
  market norm into a claim about this agreement?
- **Risk:** What's the cost of briefing leadership on an inaccurate clause read — or of
  negotiating from a "market standard" nobody sourced?
- **Human check:** Which attorney signs off before this informs the negotiation?

> **Exercise 2 output:** a one-page executive briefing, cited market background labeled as
> research, negotiation talking points marked for attorney sign-off, and a 5-prompt library.

## Prompt Library (your leave-behind)

Save these five. Anything in `[BRACKETS]` is a blank you fill in — delete the brackets too.
If you're stuck on one, use what you did in this lab: `[CONTRACT]` → *the Northwind
Distilling supply agreement*, `[STANDARD TERMS PLAYBOOK]` → *AcmeBottling_Standard_Terms.md*,
`[N]` → *three*, `[WORD COUNT]` → *300 words*. Where it says **PASTE**, paste the actual text
in.

**1. Contract summary**
```
Summarize [CONTRACT] clause by clause: [CLAUSE TYPES — e.g. payment, delivery, liability,
warranty, force majeure, termination, confidentiality, governing law, and any extra
clauses]. For each, give a one-line plain-English description and cite the clause number.
Do not add legal opinions — just describe what's there.
```
*Name the clause types you expect. It's the only way the summary can tell you one is
missing — absent text is the hardest thing for Copilot to notice on its own.*

**2. Clause comparison**
```
Compare [CONTRACT] against [STANDARD TERMS PLAYBOOK], clause by clause. For each, say
whether it Matches, is Weaker/Non-standard, or is Missing, and note the specific difference
(e.g. [AN EXAMPLE OF THE DRIFT YOU EXPECT — e.g. Net 45 vs. standard Net 30, or no liability
cap]). Flag risk level (Low/Med/High) per gap. Cite clause numbers. Do not give a legal
conclusion — flag for human review.
```
*Attach the playbook every time. Copilot holds no persistent clause library, so "our
standard terms" means nothing until it's in the chat with you.*

**3. Market research (Researcher — background, never advice)**
```
I'm preparing to negotiate [CONTRACT TYPE] between [YOUR SIDE, BY CATEGORY] and
[THEIR SIDE, BY CATEGORY]. Research general commercial norms for these terms:
[THE TERMS YOU'RE ARGUING ABOUT]. For each, tell me what is commonly treated as
market-standard, what is considered aggressive, and what tends to be traded away in
exchange for what. Prioritize [DATE RANGE] sources, cite a source for every point, and
separate what you verified from what you inferred. This is background research only — do
not give legal advice and do not tell me what any specific contract means.
```
*Describe the **category**, never your company, your counterparty, or the matter — that keeps
the question answerable and keeps your deal out of a web search. Label the output "market
background, not advice" before it touches anything else you've written.*

**4. Executive legal briefing**
```
Write a one-page executive legal briefing on [MATTER]. Structure: Overview, Key gaps vs.
standard terms (with risk levels), The [N] issues to negotiate first, Recommended next
steps. Under [WORD COUNT], plain English for a non-lawyer audience. End with a "For legal
review before action" line listing the clauses needing sign-off.
```
*"Plain English for a non-lawyer" is what makes this useful and what makes it risky. The
final line is the guardrail — never drop it to save words.*

**5. Negotiation talking points (with conclusion check)**
```
From the briefing, write talking points for the [N] highest-risk items. For each: our
position in one or two sentences and a fallback. Where market background supports a
fallback, say so and label it "market background, not advice." No legal jargon. Mark each
"draft — attorney to confirm." Then flag any sentence that states a legal conclusion, gives
advice, asserts what a clause "means" rather than what it says, or presents market
background as a fact about this agreement, and rewrite it as a neutral description plus a
review flag.
```
*Keep the second half attached to the first. Run the check as a separate prompt later and
half the time it never gets run at all.*
