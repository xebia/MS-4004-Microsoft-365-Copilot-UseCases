# MS-4004.X — Marketing & Creative Hands-On Lab
### Acme Bottling · Microsoft 365 Copilot

**Audience:** Marketing and creative team members who already use Copilot and want to move faster on research, briefs, copy, pitch decks, and campaign communications.

**Format:** ~65 minutes of hands-on

**Level:** Intermediate. We skip the basics and get straight into real Acme Bottling work.

You're launching Acme's new **500 mL recyclable PET bottle**. You'll use Copilot to research the market, build a campaign brief, write brand-safe copy, build an executive pitch deck, and write the launch-event communications — and leave with reusable prompts.

> **How to use this lab:** Just read and follow along. Nothing to install, no repo to download, no code to save. Open the files linked below and go.

---

## What you'll be able to do

- Run market and competitive research with Researcher and check its citations.
- Substantiate a marketing claim against your own data before it ships.
- Build a campaign brief from goals and proof points, and generate content from it.
- Draft an executive presentation and a sales one-pager from that brief.
- Generate slide visuals with Copilot \u2014 and know exactly where the usage-rights line sits.
- Write brand-safe copy and a launch-event communication set that stays inside approved rules.
- Engineer prompts deliberately — audience, priority, banned words, voice sample — and
  keep them in a reusable library.
- Keep a validation habit so nothing goes out without a human check.

## Learning arc

This lab moves in one direction: **prove the message, then build everything from it.**

|  | **Exercise 1 — Research, then build the brief** | **Exercise 2 — Build the pitch and the comms** |
|---|---|---|
| **You're doing** | Turning research and approved goals into a brief | Turning the brief into sales-ready assets |
| **Copilot's job** | Find market context, test the claims, structure the message | Draft the deck, one-pager, and event comms, flag risk |
| **Your job** | Decide what's evidence and what's just framing | Decide what's safe to show a customer |
| **Stakes** | Internal — only the team sees it | External — sales leadership and customers see it |
| **You leave with** | A one-page brief every claim traces back to | Four assets + a 7-prompt library |

Every task runs the same beat: **prompt → read it critically → keep what's reusable.**
Every exercise ends the same way: **Source / Assumptions / Risk / Human check.**

## What this means before anything goes public

Copilot can't guarantee brand or image fidelity, and it will restate a claim just as

assumptions, risk, and the human check.

# Exercise 1 — Research the market, then build the campaign brief

**Objective:** Ground the launch in cited market research, test the approved claims
against Acme's own data, and produce a one-page campaign brief plus three pieces of launch
copy where every claim traces back to an approved proof point.

**Scenario:** Leadership approved the new PET line. You have goals and proof points, but
no market context and no evidence the claims hold up. Build both, then turn them into a
brief the sales and creative teams can run with.

### Task 1 — Set your brand voice and clean your context

First you'll tell Copilot how to write for marketing, then scope it to this campaign only.

1. Open **Copilot Chat** and go to **Settings** (gear icon or your profile picture, top
   right) → **Personalization**.
2. Add this **custom instruction** and save:

   ```
   When I work on marketing content: write practical, confident copy in plain English.
   No hype words, no superlatives, no invented claims. Use only the proof points I give
   you, and flag anything you're unsure about instead of guessing. If you use outside
   research, label it as research and cite it — never state it as a claim. End with a note
   on what a human must review for brand tone and usage rights.
   ```

   *No Personalization option in your tenant? Paste this block at the top of your first
   prompt in each exercise instead.*
3. In Copilot Chat, start a **New chat** (the pencil or **New chat** icon at the top of
   the pane) so nothing from other campaigns bleeds in.

> **Why:** One folder, one clean chat keeps your copy on-brand and on-message. The custom
> instruction keeps the hype out without you re-typing the rule every prompt.

### Task 2 — Research the market and the competition

1. In **Copilot Chat**, open **Agents** in the left sidebar (or the agent picker above the
   prompt box) and select **Researcher**.
2. Paste this prompt and press Enter:

   ```
   I'm planning a Q4 2026 U.S. launch for a 500 mL lightweight, 100% recyclable PET bottle
   sold to mid-size beverage brands in juice, water, and kombucha. Scope it this way: the
   U.S. market, prioritizing 2024-2026 evidence, and competitor positioning at the bottle,
   preform, and packaging-supplier level. Research the current market and return: five
   drivers behind sustainable-packaging decisions for these buyers, three ways competing
   packaging suppliers position PET against glass and aluminum, and two sustainability
   claims that are common in this category but hard to substantiate. Cite a source for
   every point, and separate what you verified from what you inferred.
   ```

3. **Researcher may still ask you a question or two before it runs** — which geography,
   how recent the evidence should be, how wide to cast the competitor net. That's the agent
   working, not stalling. Answer it, or reply **"go ahead"** to let it use its best
   judgment. Either way, read what it asked: those are the scoping decisions you should be
   making *in the prompt* next time. That's the lesson, not the delay.

4. **Open two of the citations and actually read them.** Anything you can't trace to a
   real, current source doesn't make it into the brief.
5. **Stay in the same chat** and narrow the research into positioning:

   ```
   From that research, draft three positioning angles for this launch, each in one
   sentence, aimed at a packaging buyer who is under cost pressure and sustainability
   pressure at the same time. For each, name the market driver it leans on and the risk of
   leaning on it.
   ```

> **Why:** Researcher is the one step here that reaches outside your tenant, so it's the
> one place Copilot can hand you something nobody approved. Research shapes **positioning**.
> It never becomes a **claim**.

### Task 3 — Test the proof points, then build the brief

1. **Stay in the same chat.** Attach **both** `AcmeBottling_Campaign_Goals.md` and
   `AcmeBottling_Products.csv` using the paperclip icon.
2. Before writing anything, make Copilot check the claim:

   ```
   Two attachments: Acme's approved campaign input brief and our product catalog. Check
   the approved proof point "unit cost 30% below comparable glass (SKU-GB16)" against the
   catalog's unit costs for SKU-PET500 and SKU-GB16. State the actual difference and say
   plainly whether the approved wording is supported, understated, or overstated. Do not
   rewrite the claim yourself — flag it.
   ```

3. Read the answer against the catalog yourself. **The approved wording and the data don't
   agree.** Decide who owns fixing that — and note that it isn't Copilot, and it isn't you
   at 4pm on launch day.
4. **Stay in the same chat** — both files stay attached and the positioning angles from
   Task 2 are still in context. Now build the brief:

   ```
   Build a one-page campaign brief for Acme Bottling's SKU-PET500. Use the attached
   campaign input brief as the only source for claims, and the positioning angles above
   only for framing. Include: objective, target audience, three key messages built from the
   approved proof points, the featured customers (Sunrise Juice, Blue Ridge Springs,
   Prairie Kombucha), and a simple success metric. Practical, confident tone — no hype
   words, no superlatives. Do not invent claims. End with an "Assumptions & to verify" line
   that carries the cost-claim flag forward.
   ```

5. Confirm every claim traces back to the input brief, and that the flagged cost claim is
   still flagged.
6. **Save the brief as a Word file — everything in Exercise 2 reads from it.** Open
   **Word** → **Blank document**, copy the brief out of the chat and paste it in, then
   **File → Save As** → your **`Acme - PET500 Launch`** folder → name it
   **`PET500 Campaign Brief`**.

> **Why this one save matters:** Copilot's context stops at the app boundary — PowerPoint
> and Word can't see this chat. A file in your lab folder they can all read. From here on
> you'll point Copilot at the brief instead of re-explaining it, which is the habit worth
> taking back to work.

### Task 4 — Write launch copy, then engineer the prompt

1. **Stay in the same chat** (so Copilot still has the brief in context) and paste:

   ```
   Write three short pieces of launch copy from this brief: (1) a 40-word LinkedIn post,
   (2) an email subject line plus a one-sentence preview line, (3) a 60-word sales
   one-liner. Follow the brand rules: practical and confident, no hype words, no new
   claims. Use only the approved proof points. Flag anything you're unsure about instead of
   guessing.
   ```

2. Read for tone. It'll be *fine*. **Stay in the same chat** and fix the **prompt**
   instead of the output:

   ```
   Rewrite the same three pieces with four changes: the audience is a packaging buyer, not
   a consumer; lead with the lightweighting proof point, not sustainability; ban rhetorical
   questions and the words "unlock," "revolutionize," "game-changing," and "seamless"; and
   match the voice of this sample — [PASTE two or three sentences of real Acme copy you
   like]. Keep every claim identical.
   ```

3. Put v1 and v2 side by side. **The difference between them is the prompt, not the
   model.** Those four dials — *audience, priority, banned words, voice sample* — are what
   you're really saving when you save a prompt.

### Validation checkpoint

- **Source:** Which lines came from outside research, and which came from the approved
  brief? Can you tell them apart in the output?
- **Assumptions:** Any claim that isn't in the approved proof points — including anything
  the research made sound true?
- **Risk:** What happens if the unverified cost claim ships in a public post?
- **Human check:** Who approves brand tone and usage rights before anything goes live?

> **Exercise 1 output:** cited market research, three positioning angles, a substantiation
> flag on the cost claim, a one-page campaign brief, and three pieces of launch copy in two
> versions.

# Exercise 2 — Build the executive pitch and the launch comms

**Objective:** Turn the brief into a 6-slide pitch deck, a sales one-pager, and a
launch-event communication set, then run a brand-risk pass before anything leaves the team.

**Scenario:** Sales leadership wants a short deck to open PET line conversations, and
marketing is hosting a launch webinar in October. Build both from your brief — fast,
clean, on-brand.

### Task 1 — Draft the deck in PowerPoint, then generate the visuals

1. Open **PowerPoint** — press **Start** and type *PowerPoint*, or use the app launcher    (the grid icon, top-left) in Microsoft 365 on the web. Choose **Blank Presentation**, then **File → Save As** into your **`Acme - PET500 Launch`** folder and name it **`PET500 Pitch Deck`**.
2. On the **Home** tab, click the **Copilot** button (lower right corner or right of the ribbon).
3. In the prompt box type **`/`** and pick **PET500 Campaign Brief** — the Word file you
   saved in Exercise 1. That's how this pane gets the brief; it can't see your chat. Then
   add:

   ```
   Create a 6-slide executive pitch deck for Acme Bottling's new 500 mL recyclable PET
   bottle, using the referenced campaign brief as the only source for claims. Slides:
   (1) title, (2) the opportunity, (3) three key messages, (4) proof points — use only the
   approved wording from the brief and keep any claim flagged for verification marked as
   unverified, (5) featured customers, (6) call to action. Keep text tight — short bullets,
   no paragraphs. Do not add stock images or logos; leave image placeholders labeled
   "approved product photo here."
   ```

4. Review the flow. Check that the flagged cost claim didn't quietly become a confident
   bullet on slide 4. Note where you'll drop the approved product photo.
5. Now make a visual. Go to **Copilot Chat** (or **Microsoft Designer**) and start a
   **New chat** — don't reuse your campaign chat. That thread is saturated with "500 mL
   PET bottle," and image generation follows the room: ask for an abstract background in
   there and you'll very likely get a bottle. A clean chat is what makes the "no product"
   rule below actually hold. Then paste:

   ```
   Create a 16:9 background image for a product launch slide: abstract, clean, and light,
   suggesting recyclability and lightweight material — soft gradients and simple shapes.
   No text, no logos, no brand marks, and do not draw a bottle or any identifiable
   product. Premium and practical, not playful.
   ```

6. Generate two or three variations by **changing one dial at a time** — mood, color,
   composition — exactly like the copy dials in Exercise 1 Task 4. Same lesson: the
   difference between a usable image and a bad one is the prompt.
7. **Download the one you want.** Hover the image in Copilot and click **Download** (or
   right-click it → **Save image as…**). It lands in your **Downloads** folder — **move it
   into your `Acme - PET500 Launch` folder**, same as you did with the lab kit. Copy-paste
   works too, but a saved file is reusable and a reviewer can find it.
8. Put it behind the title slide: select the title slide, then **Design** tab →
   **Format Background** → **Picture or texture fill** → **Insert** → **From a File**, and
   pick the image you just saved. *(Or **Insert** tab → **Pictures** → **This Device**,
   then right-click the image → **Send to Back**.)* Keep the text readable — if you can't
   read it, the image is decoration you don't need.

> **The line that matters — read this before you use anything you generated.** A generated
> image can be an **abstract background or an internal concept**. It can never be **the
> product**. Copilot has never seen the PET500, so anything it draws that resembles a
> bottle is a bottle that doesn't exist — wrong neck finish, wrong label, wrong
> proportions — and showing it to a buyer misrepresents what they'd actually receive. The
> "approved product photo here" placeholder from step 3 stays a placeholder until a human
> drops in the real asset. Same rule for logos, customer brands, and anything resembling a
> certification mark.

> **If image generation isn't available in your tenant,** it's licensed separately from
> Copilot Chat in some setups. Skip to Task 2 — you've already got the prompt, and the
> rule above is the part that matters. Or use **Designer** on the web with the same prompt.

> **Brand-safe reminder:** Copilot may not match brand templates or images perfectly.
> That's expected — you place approved assets and review before sharing.

### Task 2 — Turn the brief into a one-pager

1. Open **Word** — press **Start** and type *Word*, or use the app launcher (the grid icon,
   top-left) in Microsoft 365 on the web. Choose **Blank document**, then **File → Save As**
   into your **`Acme - PET500 Launch`** folder and name it **`PET500 One-Pager`**.
2. On the **Home** tab, click the **Copilot** button (lower right corner or right of the
   ribbon) to open the Copilot pane.
3. In the prompt box type **`/`** and pick **PET500 Campaign Brief**, then add:

   ```
   From the referenced campaign brief, write a one-page sales enablement sheet for the
   PET500 launch: a short intro, the three key messages, the proof points as a tight bullet
   list, and a "who to call" line naming the featured customers' segments. Under 250 words,
   no hype. Carry forward any claim the brief flagged as unverified. End with an
   "Assumptions & to verify" line.
   ```

4. **The answer appears in the Copilot pane, not in your document.** Click **Copy** under
   the response and paste it into the page (or **Insert** if your version offers it).
   Nothing is in the document until you do.
5. Tighten, confirm the verify line is present, and **save**.

> **Note what you didn't do:** you never re-pasted the brief. One file, referenced from two
> different apps. That's the pattern — put the source of truth in the folder once, then
> point at it.

### Task 3 — Write the launch event communications

Same message, four channels. This is where most marketing time actually goes.

1. **Go back to your campaign chat** — the Exercise 1 one that still has the brief in it.
   Not the clean chat you used for the image, and not the Copilot pane in Word or
   PowerPoint. Paste:

   ```
   Acme is running a 30-minute PET500 launch webinar in October for existing customers and
   their packaging buyers. Using my campaign brief, write the communication set: (1) a
   90-word invitation with one clear value line and one call to action, (2) a 40-word
   reminder for the day before, (3) a 60-word thank-you follow-up with a single next step,
   and (4) a 3-line Teams post the account managers can share internally. Keep the meaning
   and every claim identical across all four — only the format changes. Practical tone, no
   hype, approved proof points only.
   ```

2. Attach `AcmeBottling_Customers.csv` using the paperclip icon, then target it:

   ```
   Using the attached customers file, tell me which segments and regions this invitation
   should be tailored for, and rewrite the invitation's opening line for the juice, water,
   and kombucha segments. Name the account manager who owns each of the three featured
   accounts. Do not change any claim.
   ```

3. Check the account managers against the file yourself — a misattributed account manager
   in a customer-facing invite is a small error with a very visible blast radius.

### Task 4 — Run the brand and usage-rights pass

1. **Stay in your campaign chat** — the four-channel communication set from Task 3 is
   already there, so that's one less thing to move.
2. Attach your two saved files with the paperclip icon: **`PET500 Pitch Deck`** and
   **`PET500 One-Pager`**. Then paste:

   ```
   Review the two attached files and the communication set you wrote above against these
   brand rules: practical and confident tone, no hype words, no claims outside the approved
   proof points, no stock imagery or logos, no generated image that depicts the product
   itself, and no unverified claim stated as fact. Return a table: item, the rule it may
   break, and the fix. If you are not certain, say so rather than approving it.
   ```

3. This is a *first pass*, not an approval. A human still signs off.

> **Three assets, no copy-paste.** Because you saved the deck and the one-pager into the
> lab folder, the review can read them directly. That's the whole reason the folder rule
> exists.

### Task 5 — Save your prompt library

1. Create a new **Word** doc titled **"Acme Marketing — Prompt Library"**.
2. Paste in the seven prompts from the
   [Prompt Library](#prompt-library-your-leave-behind) section below.
3. Save it somewhere you'll actually find it again.

This is your leave-behind. Swap in the next product or campaign and reuse.

### Validation checkpoint

- **Source:** Did the deck, one-pager, and comms stay inside the approved brief and proof
  points — or did outside research leak in as fact?
- **Assumptions:** Did Copilot add any claim, image, logo, date, or account owner it
  shouldn't have?
- **Risk:** What's the cost of shipping off-brand visuals or an unapproved claim to a
  customer invite list?
- **Human check:** Who does the final brand + usage-rights review before this goes out?

> **Exercise 2 output:** a 6-slide pitch deck, a sales enablement one-pager, a four-channel
> launch communication set, a brand-risk list, and a 7-prompt library.

## Facilitator notes

- **Data hygiene:** The clean-folder step (Ex.1 Task 1) is the teachable habit — one
  campaign, one folder, one clean chat.
- **The planted claim — know this before you teach it:** the approved proof point says
  PET500 costs *30% below* SKU-GB16, but the catalog shows **$0.15 vs $0.55 (≈73% lower)**.
  The approved wording and the data disagree. Ex.1 Task 3 exists for this. Watch for two
  failure modes: Copilot silently "correcting" the claim to 73%, and learners accepting
  either number without opening the catalog. The right answer is *flag it and escalate* —
  marketing doesn't get to restate an approved claim, and neither does Copilot.
- **Source separation:** Ex.1 Task 2 pulls from outside the tenant; Ex.1 Task 3 onward is
  approved-source-only. Say the line out loud: **research shapes positioning, proof points
  become claims.** If a learner's brief cites a market statistic Researcher found, that's
  the teaching moment, not a mistake to skip past.
- **Researcher will probably ask before it runs.** Expect a clarifying round on geography,
  recency, and competitor breadth even though the prompt pre-scopes all three. Tell the
  room up front so nobody thinks they broke it, and don't let the group spend more than a
  minute there — **"go ahead"** is a perfectly good answer. Learners will also get slightly
  different results from each other; that's the nature of the agent, not an error.
- **Prompt engineering:** Ex.1 Task 4 is the point of the whole lab for some groups. Have
  two people read v1 and v2 aloud. The four dials — audience, priority, banned words, voice
  sample — are the reusable lesson; the copy is disposable.
- **Brand reality:** Reinforce that Copilot won't perfectly follow brand templates or
  generate brand-safe imagery. Teach method and source control. Ex.2 Task 4 is a
  first-pass filter, never an approval.
- **Images — expect this to be the most popular part, and the easiest to get wrong.**
  Ex.2 Task 1 steps 5–7 exist to satisfy the "can Copilot make my slide visuals?" question
  *and* to draw the line in the same breath. The answer is yes for **abstract backgrounds
  and internal concepts**, never for **the product, a logo, a customer's brand, or a
  certification mark**. If someone generates a plausible-looking PET bottle, put it on the
  screen — it's the best teaching moment in the lab. Ask the room what's wrong with the
  neck finish, then ask what happens if a buyer orders from that picture. Nine times out
  of ten the cause is that they reused the campaign chat instead of starting a clean one —
  which makes the point about context hygiene better than any slide could. Also expect
  tenant variation: image generation is licensed separately in some setups, so have the
  group share one screen rather than stalling.
- **Context does not follow learners into Word or PowerPoint — and the fix is the lesson.**
  Copilot's context stops at the app boundary, so the lab has learners **save the brief as
  a Word file** at the end of Ex.1 Task 3 and then **reference it with `/`** from both
  PowerPoint and Word. Make this explicit when you teach it: the alternative is pasting the
  brief into every prompt, which is what most people do and why they think Copilot "forgets."
  If someone skips the save, their deck and one-pager will be confidently invented from
  nothing — show that to the room, then have them do it properly.
- **Expect a web/desktop split in Ex.2.** PowerPoint on the web has no **Outline View**, so
  the "copy the deck as text" step gives a Copilot-pane alternative that works in both.
  Ribbon positions for the **Copilot** button also differ between web and desktop \u2014 don't
  let anyone conclude their tenant is broken.
- **Capture agent ideas:** If someone imagines "a brand-checker that reviews every draft
  automatically," that's a real agent opportunity — capture it and route to **Chris
  Bartlow**. Keep this lab current-state Copilot.

  |---|---|---|---|
